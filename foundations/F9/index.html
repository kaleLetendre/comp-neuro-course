    Lesson spec¶

    Goal: Establish synaptic weights as variables changing under local activity-dependent rules rather than fixed parameters. Derive the basic Hebbian rule's instability and the normalisation fixes, then show that running such a rule on a population is doing statistics — Oja's rule extracts the principal component of the input covariance. Contrast with supervised rules that need an external teacher.

    Scope — what the lesson covers
    • LTP and LTD as bidirectional weight change; STDP's timing window as the biological grounding for rate-based rules
    • The basic Hebbian rule, its unbounded growth, and subtractive versus multiplicative normalisation as two fixes producing two different weight distributions
    • Covariance and BCM rules, with BCM's sliding threshold as its stability mechanism
    • Oja's rule converging on the principal eigenvector of the input covariance; ocular dominance and receptive-field formation as what this computes developmentally
    • The delta rule and the perceptron as the supervised contrast case
    • Not covered: reward-based three-factor rules (F10)

    You should be able to answer: 1. A presynaptic spike leads the postsynaptic spike by 10 ms — is the synapse strengthened or weakened, and roughly what is the half-width in milliseconds of the STDP window it falls inside? 2. For an unnormalised Hebbian rule, what happens to the weight magnitude as time goes to infinity, and why does that make the rule implausible on its own? 3. Subtractive and multiplicative normalisation both bound total weight — which drives individual weights to saturate at their bounds, and which leaves a smoother distribution? 4. For Oja's rule, what vector does the weight converge to, expressed in terms of the input covariance matrix? 5. Write the delta-rule update for a linear unit with target d and output y, and name the one input it needs that Hebbian, covariance and BCM rules do not.

    Feeds: the Bridge checkpoint, A4 (short-term plasticity and eligibility traces), B4 (developmental pruning builds on the competition material), F10 (reward-based learning as the third regime).

    Done when: For any rule in this lesson you states its update equation, what it converges to or diverges toward, and whether it needs a teacher signal — without consulting the chapter.

    Coursework¶

    Consume, then apply, then refine: guided reading (Piece 3) → derivation (Piece 1) → simulation (Piece 2) → written explanation (Piece 4). Pieces are numbered by type, not sequence.

    Type: derivation · Time: 60-75 min

    Learning goals - Solve the linear ODE for an unconstrained rate-based Hebbian synapse and show the weight has no finite steady state. - Derive the general fixed-point condition for Oja's rule and show it reduces to the eigenvector equation of the input covariance. - Linearize Oja's dynamics around two candidate fixed points and use the sign of the resulting rates to determine which is stable. - State, in one paragraph, why Oja's rule finds specifically the top eigenvector and not any eigenvector.

    Task

    Part (a) — the instability. A linear unit has output \(y = wx\) driven by a presynaptic input with constant variance \(\sigma_x^2 = 4\). It follows the basic (rate-based) Hebb rule \(\dot w = \eta \langle yx \rangle = \eta \sigma_x^2 w\), with \(\eta = 0.1\). (i) Solve this ODE for \(w(t)\) given \(w(0) = 0.01\). (ii) Evaluate \(w(t)\) at \(t=5\) and \(t=20\) (time in whatever units make \(\eta\) dimensionless-per-unit-time). (iii) State the limit as \(t \to \infty\), and explain in one or two sentences why this is biologically impossible for a real synapse (tie it to LTP saturation).

    Part (b) — the fixed point. Now a linear unit has a 2-D input with covariance matrix \(C = \mathrm{diag}(3, 1)\) (so \(e_1=(1,0)\) and \(e_2=(0,1)\) are already its eigenvectors, with eigenvalues \(\lambda_1=3\), \(\lambda_2=1\)). Oja's rule is \(\dot w = \eta\left(Cw - (w^\top C w)\,w\right)\). (i) Show algebraically that setting \(\dot w = 0\) requires \(Cw^* = (w^{*\top}Cw^*)\,w^*\), and that for any unit-norm \(w^*\) this is exactly the eigenvector equation \(Cw^* = \lambda w^*\) with \(\lambda = w^{*\top}Cw^*\). (ii) Take \(\eta = 0.05\). Linearize around \(w^* = (1,0)\): write \(w = (1+\delta_1, \delta_2)\), substitute into \(\dot w\), drop terms of order \(\delta^2\) and higher, and derive the two first-order rates \(\dot\delta_1 = a\,\delta_1\) and \(\dot\delta_2 = b\,\delta_2\). (iii) Repeat the same linearization around \(w^* = (0,1)\), writing \(w=(\delta_1, 1+\delta_2)\), and derive the corresponding rates \(c\,\delta_1\) and \(d\,\delta_2\). (iv) Using the four signed rates from (ii)-(iii), classify each fixed point (stable node vs. saddle) and state the general rule connecting the sign of the transverse rate to \((\lambda_i - \lambda_1)\).

    Deliverable

    A written derivation (typed or scanned handwriting) covering (a)(i)-(iii) and (b)(i)-(iv), showing every algebraic step — not just final expressions — plus the four numeric rates from (b) and a one-paragraph statement of the general result: "Among unit vectors, Oja's rule's only asymptotically stable fixed point is the eigenvector of the largest eigenvalue of \(C\)."

    Rubric — how this is marked
    Band Criteria
    Strong All sub-parts solved correctly with shown algebra; both fixed points correctly classified with correct signed rates; closing paragraph correctly generalizes beyond the diagonal example (states the result in terms of \(\lambda_i - \lambda_1\), not just "it worked for this matrix").
    Adequate Part (a) fully correct. Part (b)(i) correct. At least one of the two linearizations in (b)(ii)/(b)(iii) correct with correct sign; final classification of at least \(w^*=(1,0)\) as stable is correct, even if the saddle argument is thin.
    Not yet Part (a) has a sign or algebra error changing the qualitative conclusion (e.g. concludes decay instead of growth), or part (b) never reaches a signed rate — only asserts "eigenvectors are fixed points" without doing the linearization.

    Type: simulation · Time: 90-120 min

    Learning goals - Verify, numerically, that Oja's rule converges onto the principal eigenvector of a known input covariance — computed independently by hand. - Watch plain unnormalized Hebbian learning diverge on the same data and characterize the divergence quantitatively. - Reproduce the qualitative difference between subtractive and multiplicative normalization: saturation-prone vs. smooth weight distributions.

    Task

    Write one self-contained Python script (numpy only, no downloads, under 100 lines) with three parts. Exact parameters below are mandatory — do not substitute your own, since results are checked numerically.

    Part A — Oja's rule finds the eigenvector. 1. By hand, before writing any code: for \(C = \begin{pmatrix}2 & 1\\1 & 1\end{pmatrix}\), compute both eigenvalues and the eigenvector belonging to the larger eigenvalue, normalized to unit length. Show the characteristic polynomial and the null-space computation — don't just quote a numerical eigensolver. 2. Generate data: rng = np.random.RandomState(0), Z = rng.randn(5000, 2), Cholesky-factor \(C\) by hand as \(L=\begin{pmatrix}\sqrt2 & 0\\ 1/\sqrt2 & \sqrt{0.5}\end{pmatrix}\) (verify \(LL^\top=C\) yourself), then X = Z @ L.T. 3. Implement Oja's rule as an online update over all 5000 rows in order: \(y = w\cdot x\), \(w \leftarrow w + \eta(yx - y^2w)\), with \(\eta=0.01\), \(w_0=(1,0)\). 4. Report the final \(w\), \(\|w\|\), and the cosine similarity between final \(w\) and your hand-computed eigenvector from step 1.

    Part B — plain Hebbian diverges. 5. On the same X from Part A (same order), run the unnormalized rule \(w \leftarrow w + \eta\,y\,x\), \(\eta=0.01\), \(w_0=(0.05, 0)\). 6. Record \(\|w\|\) after steps 100, 500, 1000, and 5000 (0-indexed: "after step \(k\)" = after processing \(k\) rows). Report the final normalized direction \(w/\|w\|\) and compare it, in one sentence, to Part A's converged direction.

    Part C — subtractive vs. multiplicative normalization. 7. Build 10 synapses' worth of input: rng2 = np.random.RandomState(1), gains = np.linspace(0.7, 1.3, 10), base = rng2.randn(8000), Xc = gains[None,:]*base[:,None] + 0.3*rng2.randn(8000,10), then shift so the minimum entry is exactly \(0.1\): Xc = Xc - Xc.min() + 0.1. 8. Implement subtractive normalization: each step, \(y=w\cdot x\), \(\Delta w_i = \eta\,y\,(x_i - \bar x)\) (mean-subtracted, so \(\sum_i \Delta w_i = 0\) before clipping), then clip every \(w_i\) to \([0, 2]\), then re-distribute any excess/deficit from clipping among the not-yet-saturated units so that \(\sum_i w_i = 10\) exactly is restored each step. \(\eta = 0.01\), \(w_0 = (1,1,\dots,1)\). 9. Implement multiplicative normalization: each step, \(w \leftarrow w + \eta\,y\,x\), then rescale \(w \leftarrow w\cdot(10/\sum_i w_i)\). Same \(\eta\) and \(w_0\). 10. Report, for each rule: the final weight vector, how many entries are at or within \(10^{-3}\) of a bound (0 or 2), and the standard deviation across the 10 weights.

    Deliverable

    The script itself, plus a short (5-10 sentence) results note reporting: the cosine similarity number from Part A and how many degrees off-axis that implies; the four \(\|w\|\) values from Part B and the qualitative growth shape; and the saturation-count / std-dev pair for each rule in Part C, with a one-line statement of which normalization scheme is the "winner-take-all" one.

    Rubric — how this is marked
    Band Criteria
    Strong All three parts implemented exactly to spec (correct RNG calls, correct constants); Part A cosine similarity ≥ 0.99 reported and correctly matched against an independently-derived hand eigenvector (not copied from code output); Part B values match expected order of magnitude at steps 100/500/1000, correctly identified as exponential blow-up; Part C correctly shows more saturated entries and higher std for subtractive than multiplicative, with the right qualitative read on which is "smoother."
    Adequate All three parts run and produce a result in the right qualitative direction (Oja converges, Hebb diverges, subtractive is more saturated/spread than multiplicative), but one part has a parameter deviation (wrong seed, wrong \(\eta\), wrong \(w_0\)) that you didn't catch, or the hand-eigenvector check in Part A is missing/wrong even though the code result is fine.
    Not yet Any part is missing, or Oja's rule fails to converge (cosine similarity well below 0.99, e.g. from an update-rule sign error), or the Part C rules are not actually distinguishable in the reported output (same std, same saturation), indicating the two update rules were implemented identically.

    Type: guided reading · Time: 45-60 min

    Learning goals - Locate, in the primary text, the biological grounding (LTP/LTD, STDP) for the rate-based rules used elsewhere in this lesson. - Extract the exact mathematical form of each rule (Hebb, covariance, BCM, Oja) as the text presents it, well enough to state it from memory afterward. - Identify what distinguishes the supervised rules from everything before them in the chapter.

    Task

    Read D&A ch. 8, sections Introduction, Synaptic Plasticity Rules, Unsupervised Learning, and Supervised Learning, in that order. Answer the following from the text — do not answer from memory of this course's earlier lessons or from outside sources:

    1. (Introduction) What two directions of synaptic weight change do LTP and LTD correspond to, and roughly what timescale separates "short-term" from "long-term" potentiation as the chapter frames it?
    2. (Introduction or Synaptic Plasticity Rules, wherever the chapter places it) A presynaptic spike precedes the postsynaptic spike by 10 ms. According to the STDP timing window as the text describes it, is the synapse strengthened or weakened, and what is the approximate half-width (in ms) of the window this falls inside?
    3. (Synaptic Plasticity Rules) Write the basic Hebb rule in the rate-based form the chapter gives (in terms of presynaptic rate, postsynaptic rate, and a learning rate). What property of this equation's right-hand side is responsible for unbounded growth?
    4. (Synaptic Plasticity Rules) How does the covariance rule change the basic Hebb rule so that it can produce LTD (weight decrease), not just LTP? What is subtracted, and from which factor (pre- or post-synaptic, or both)?
    5. (Synaptic Plasticity Rules) What is the BCM rule's sliding threshold usually called, and how does making it depend on a running average of recent postsynaptic activity keep the rule from diverging the way the basic Hebb rule does?
    6. (Unsupervised Learning) What quantity, defined from the input's second-order statistics, does Oja's rule converge to extract? What two developmental phenomena does the chapter offer as biological instances of this computation (one about which eye drives a cortical cell, one about spatial receptive field structure)?
    7. (Supervised Learning) Write the delta rule's weight update for a linear unit with target output \(d\) and actual output \(y\). What single quantity does this rule require that the Hebb, covariance, BCM, and Oja rules never need?
    8. (Supervised Learning) What condition on the training data does the perceptron convergence theorem require for the perceptron learning procedure to be guaranteed to converge?

    Deliverable

    Written answers to all 8 questions, each citing which named section it came from, in your own words (not copied sentences).

    Completion bar¶

Concretely: they can reproduce the STDP direction/timing fact (criterion 1), explain in their own words why unnormalized Hebbian learning has no finite steady state (criterion 2, backed by their own Piece 1 numbers), state which of the two normalization schemes saturates individual weights and which doesn't (criterion 3, backed by their own Piece 2C numbers), state that Oja's rule converges to the top eigenvector of the input covariance (criterion 4, backed by their own Piece 2A cosine similarity), and write the delta rule while naming the teacher signal as what separates it from everything else in the lesson (criterion 5).
