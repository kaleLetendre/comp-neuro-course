## Piece 1 — Eigenmode analysis of a linear recurrent network

**Type:** derivation · **Time:** 90-120 min

**Learning goals**
- Derive the effective time constant and steady-state gain of a single eigenmode of a linear recurrent network, from the network's defining ODE.
- State and justify the stability condition for the full network in terms of the eigenvalues of the recurrent weight matrix.
- Reduce a linearised excitatory-inhibitory network to a 2x2 eigenvalue problem and derive the algebraic condition that separates damped oscillatory return to equilibrium from monotonic decay.

**Task**

Part A — single-population eigenmode. Take the standard linear recurrent network equation from D&A §7.3:
tau du/dt = -u + h + W u,
where u(t) is the vector of population firing rates, h is the (constant, step) feedforward drive, and W is the recurrent weight matrix. Let e_a be an eigenvector of W with real eigenvalue lambda_a, and suppose the input is aligned with that eigenvector: h = h_a e_a, u(0) = 0.

1. Write u(t) = c(t) e_a, substitute, and reduce the vector ODE to a scalar ODE for c(t). Solve it in closed form.
2. Read off the effective time constant tau_eff(lambda_a) and the steady-state value c_ss.
3. Define the amplification factor A(lambda_a) as the ratio of c_ss to the steady-state value the same input would produce with W = 0 (feedforward-only). Give A(lambda_a) as a function of lambda_a alone.
4. Evaluate tau_eff and A at lambda_a = 0.8 with tau = 10 ms. State in words what happens to tau_eff as lambda_a -> 1^-, and why lambda_a = 1 is a singular point of your formula.
5. Generalise: for an N-dimensional W with eigenvalues lambda_1, ..., lambda_N (assume W diagonalisable), state the condition on {lambda_a} under which every mode of the network is stable (bounded as t -> infinity for bounded h). Justify it from the sign of (1 - lambda_a) in your part-2 solution.

Part B — excitatory-inhibitory 2x2 reduction. Consider the linearised two-population E-I system from D&A §7.5, written in the same form as Part A with u = (v_E, v_I)^T and a single shared tau:
tau du/dt = -u + h + W u, W = [[w_EE, -w_EI], [w_IE, -w_II]], all four w's >= 0.

6. For W_1 = [[0.5, -1.0], [1.5, -0.5]], compute trace(W_1) and det(W_1), form the characteristic polynomial lambda^2 - trace(W_1) lambda + det(W_1) = 0, and solve for the eigenvalues. State whether they are real or complex.
7. Repeat for W_2 = [[0.5, -1.0], [0.2, -0.5]].
8. From your part-1 solution, the decay/growth rate of a mode is governed by (lambda_a - 1)/tau. For each of W_1 and W_2, compute the eigenvalues of (W - I) and state (i) whether the network is stable, and (ii) whether its return to equilibrium after a small perturbation is monotonic or has a damped oscillatory component. Give the general algebraic rule (in terms of trace and det of the 2x2 W, or equivalently the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial) that decides real vs. complex eigenvalues, independent of the specific numbers.
9. For the complex case, give the angular frequency of the decaying oscillation (omega, in rad/ms) and the decay time constant, using tau = 10 ms.

**Deliverable**

A written derivation (LaTeX, Markdown with math, or clearly legible scanned handwriting) covering all 9 steps, with every intermediate line of algebra shown — not just final formulas. Final numeric answers for steps 4, 6, 7, 9 must be stated explicitly, not left as unevaluated expressions.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |

Correctly identifies 1 - lambda_a as the mode's decay rate and derives A(lambda_a) = 1/(1 - lambda_a) from first principles (not by pattern-matching to a remembered formula). Correctly states the general stability condition as max_a Re(lambda_a) < 1 (not just "lambda < 1" for a single real eigenvalue). Correctly derives and states the trace/det (or discriminant) rule for real vs. complex eigenvalues of a general 2x2 matrix, then applies it to both W_1 and W_2. |
| Adequate | Steps 1-4 and 6-8 correct with correct final numbers, but the general N-dimensional stability condition (step 5) is stated loosely (e.g. "eigenvalues less than 1" without addressing complex eigenvalues via their real part), or the general trace/det rule in step 8 is not stated explicitly (only the two worked numeric cases are done). |
| Not yet | Arithmetic errors in the closed-form solution, effective time constant, or eigenvalue computations; amplification factor derived by guessing rather than substitution; oscillatory vs. monotonic distinction not tied to real vs. complex eigenvalues. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only)

Part A:
1. Substituting u = c(t) e_a into tau du/dt = -u + h + Wu gives tau (dc/dt) e_a = -c e_a + h_a e_a + c lambda_a e_a (using W e_a = lambda_a e_a), i.e. tau dc/dt = -(1 - lambda_a) c + h_a. This is a first-order linear ODE with solution c(t) = [h_a / (1 - lambda_a)] (1 - exp(-t (1-lambda_a)/tau)), given c(0) = 0.
2. tau_eff = tau / (1 - lambda_a). c_ss = h_a / (1 - lambda_a).
3. Feedforward-only (W = 0) steady state is c_ss^FF = h_a. So A(lambda_a) = c_ss / c_ss^FF = 1 / (1 - lambda_a).
4. lambda_a = 0.8, tau = 10 ms: tau_eff = 10 / 0.2 = 50 ms. A(0.8) = 1/0.2 = 5. As lambda_a -> 1^-, (1 - lambda_a) -> 0^+, so tau_eff -> +infinity and A -> +infinity: the mode relaxes arbitrarily slowly and its gain diverges (marginal stability). At lambda_a = 1 exactly the formula divides by zero because the mode is undamped (a pure integrator of h_a); for lambda_a > 1, (1 - lambda_a) < 0 and the "time constant" becomes negative, i.e. the solution is a growing exponential, not a decaying one.
5. Each mode's homogeneous solution decays as exp(-(1 - lambda_a) t / tau) (or, for complex lambda_a, exp(-(1 - Re(lambda_a)) t / tau) times an oscillatory factor). Boundedness requires the real part of the exponent's coefficient to be negative: Re(1 - lambda_a) > 0 for every a, i.e. max_a Re(lambda_a) < 1. This is the stability condition (answers acceptance criterion 2).

Part B:
6. W_1: trace = 0.5 + (-0.5) = 0. det = (0.5)(-0.5) - (-1.0)(1.5) = -0.25 - (-1.5) = 1.25. Characteristic equation lambda^2 + 1.25 = 0 -> lambda^2 = -1.25 -> lambda = +-i*sqrt(1.25) = +-1.11803i. Complex (purely imaginary here since trace = 0).
7. W_2: trace = 0.5 + (-0.5) = 0. det = (0.5)(-0.5) - (-1.0)(0.2) = -0.25 + 0.2 = -0.05. Characteristic equation lambda^2 - 0.05 = 0 -> lambda = +-sqrt(0.05) = +-0.223607. Real.
8. Eigenvalues of (W - I) are just (eigenvalues of W) - 1, since W and I share eigenvectors here (I commutes with everything). W_1: (W_1 - I) eigenvalues = -1 +- 1.11803i, both real part -1 < 0 -> stable, and nonzero imaginary part -> damped oscillatory return. W_2: (W_2 - I) eigenvalues = -0.776393 and -1.223607, both real and negative -> stable, and both eigenvalues real -> monotonic (non-oscillatory) exponential decay, as a sum of two decaying exponentials with different rates. General rule: for a 2x2 matrix with characteristic polynomial lambda^2 - (trace)lambda + det = 0, the discriminant is trace^2 - 4 det. Discriminant < 0 -> complex conjugate eigenvalues -> damped oscillatory decay (given stability). Discriminant >= 0 -> real eigenvalues -> monotonic decay (a sum of exponentials). This directly answers acceptance criterion 3.
9. For W_1, eigenvalues of (W_1 - I) are -1 +- 1.11803i, interpreted as (1/tau)*(that value) in the exponent of exp((lambda-1)t/tau): decay rate = 1/tau = 1/10 ms = 0.1 /ms -> decay time constant = 10 ms; angular frequency omega = 1.11803/tau = 0.111803 rad/ms -> period = 2*pi/omega = 56.20 ms.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: Point to D&A's substitution of u = c(t)e_a into the ODE; do not perform the substitution for you.
- Hint 2: If they are stuck on the stability condition for step 5, ask "what does the sign of the real part of the exponent's coefficient control, growth or decay?" — do not state the condition.

## Piece 2 — Verifying eigenmode predictions in a linear recurrent network, then breaking it

**Type:** simulation · **Time:** 75-100 min

**Learning goals**
- Build a linear recurrent network whose weight matrix has a known, controlled eigenstructure, and recover that eigenstructure numerically.
- Verify the effective-time-constant and steady-state-amplification formulas from Piece 1 against direct numerical integration, per eigenmode.
- Observe the stability boundary directly (an eigenvalue crossing 1 produces unbounded growth) and observe that a saturating nonlinearity restores boundedness without restoring linearity's clean per-mode formulas.

**Task**

Construct a 3-neuron linear recurrent network with weight matrix
W = 0.8 (e1 e1^T) + 0.3 (e2 e2^T) + (-0.5) (e3 e3^T),
using the fixed orthonormal vectors
e1 = (1,1,1)/sqrt(3), e2 = (1,-1,0)/sqrt(2), e3 = (1,1,-2)/sqrt(6).
(These three vectors are mutually orthogonal and unit-norm; you may verify this by hand before coding.) This construction guarantees W is symmetric with exact eigenvalues 0.8, 0.3, -0.5 and eigenvectors e1, e2, e3.

Simulate tau du/dt = -u + h + Wu by explicit Euler integration with tau = 10 ms, dt = 0.1 ms, for at least 300 ms, starting from u(0) = 0.

Part 1: For each eigenvector e_a in turn, drive the network with h = 1.0 * e_a (a step input aligned with that eigenvector, held constant). Project u(t) onto e_a at every time step. Record the simulated steady-state value and the simulated time to reach 63.2% of that steady-state value (the empirical effective time constant). Compare both against the analytic tau_eff(lambda_a) and steady-state amplification formulas from Piece 1.

Part 2: Rebuild W with the first eigenvalue changed from 0.8 to 1.2 (keep 0.3 and -0.5). Drive with h = 1.0 * e1 as before and simulate for 200 ms. Confirm the projection onto e1 grows rather than saturates, and compare its growth to the analytic solution from Piece 1's step-1 formula evaluated at lambda_a = 1.2 (same closed form, now with 1 - lambda_a < 0).

Part 3: Keep the unstable W from Part 2 (lambda_1 = 1.2). Add a saturating nonlinearity: replace the recurrent drive Wu with an elementwise cap, f(x) = r_max * tanh(x / r_max) with r_max = 5.0, applied to the vector Wu before it enters the ODE. Simulate for 400 ms with the same h = 1.0 * e1. Confirm the projection onto e1 now approaches a finite steady value instead of diverging.

Write this as under ~100 lines of self-contained Python using only numpy (no downloads, no external data). Print a small table for Part 1 (lambda, predicted tau_eff, predicted steady-state, simulated steady-state, simulated tau_eff) and the key checkpoint values for Parts 2 and 3.

**Deliverable**

The `.py` script, plus its printed output, plus 3-5 sentences per part stating whether the numerical result matched the analytic prediction from Piece 1 (and by how much, if not exact).

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | W constructed correctly (numpy's eigendecomposition recovers 0.8, 0.3, -0.5 and eigenvectors matching e1, e2, e3 up to sign); Part 1 table matches analytic predictions to within a few percent for all three modes; Part 2 shows clear unbounded growth matched against the analytic exponential; Part 3 shows convergence to a finite value and you correctly identifies that the saturating network no longer obeys the simple 1/(1-lambda) amplification law because superposition (linearity) has been broken. |
| Adequate | Part 1 and Part 2 done correctly with correct qualitative conclusions, but Part 3 only shows "it doesn't blow up" without engaging the amplification-formula-breaks-down point, or the effective-time-constant measurement method (63.2% threshold) is applied inconsistently across modes. |
| Not yet | W's eigenvalues/eigenvectors not verified against the target values before use; input not actually aligned with an eigenvector (so the projection mixes modes and the comparison to Piece 1 is invalid); Part 2's instability not connected back to the sign of (1 - lambda_a); Part 3 nonlinearity applied in a way that makes it a different network (e.g. saturating u instead of the recurrent drive) without noting the difference. |

4. 5. Expected: as the largest eigenvalue approaches 1 (the stability boundary), the text describes the network's effective time constant growing without bound (slower and slower relaxation) and its steady-state gain/amplification growing without bound, i.e. the system becomes increasingly slow and increasingly sensitive right at the edge of instability.
6. Expected: variables are the excitatory population rate v_E (or u_E) and inhibitory population rate v_I (or u_I). 7. Expected: stability requires the eigenvalues of the linearised system's dynamics matrix to have real part less than the relevant threshold (equivalently, real part of (W - I)/tau negative); the text ties oscillatory vs. non-oscillatory decay to whether those eigenvalues are complex or real, i.e. it is implicit in the eigenvalues (via the discriminant of the characteristic polynomial) rather than stated as a separately named "oscillation condition."
8. The grader should accept only the stated definition (a function of the state used to define update probabilities / the Boltzmann-like distribution) and should flag any answer that goes further into describing stable configurations, minima, or memory states as out of scope for this question.
9. Expected: a commonly cited example is winner-take-all selection (or, alternatively, contrast/gain normalization) — picking out the single strongest input component while suppressing the others, or keeping total output activity within a bounded range regardless of overall input strength. **Grader key** (teacher AI only)

(a) Correct explanation: W's eigenvectors are directions that W maps onto themselves (only rescaled), so if the state is decomposed along them, u(t) = sum_a c_a(t) e_a, the coupled ODE tau du/dt = -u + h + Wu separates into N independent scalar ODEs, one per a, each of the form tau dc_a/dt = -(1-lambda_a)c_a + h_a. Every mode evolves independently, and each is a single first-order linear ODE fully described by one number (lambda_a) together with the corresponding input component h_a. Hence the whole network's transient and steady-state behaviour, for any input, is predictable from the list {lambda_a} without further simulation — you only need one eigenvalue per mode and its projected drive.

(b) Should state: with lambda=0.8 and tau=10 ms, tau_eff = 10/(1-0.8) = 50 ms — the network takes 5x longer to settle along that eigenvector than an isolated (non-recurrent) neuron with the same tau would. The steady-state amplification factor is 1/(1-0.8) = 5 — a unit input along that eigenvector produces 5x the steady-state response it would produce with no recurrence. "Selective" means this factor of 5 applies only to input aligned with that particular eigenvector; a component aligned with the lambda=-0.5 eigenvector is instead attenuated to 0.667x. The network is not amplifying everything uniformly — it discriminates between input patterns based on their eigenvector content.

(c) Acceptable pair (any two well-justified computations count; these are the standard pair):
- Winner-take-all / selective suppression of weaker inputs: violates superposition. In a linear network, the response to a sum of two eigenmode components is exactly the sum of each component's independently-amplified response (that is what "linear" means) — there is no mechanism by which a stronger component's growth can suppress a weaker one; their amplification factors 1/(1-lambda) are fixed numbers independent of each other's presence. A saturating network's nonlinearity couples the modes' fates (e.g. through shared bounded resources or divisive-type interaction), so a strong response can suppress a weaker one — impossible under pure superposition.
- Gain control / normalization (bounded output regardless of overall input magnitude): violates homogeneity (scale invariance). A linear system satisfies f(c*x) = c*f(x) for any scalar c — doubling the input exactly doubles the output, with no ceiling. There is no way to keep the response within a fixed bound as input grows arbitrarily large unless the input-output relationship itself compresses large inputs, which is precisely what a saturating function (and only a nonlinear one) does.

Any answer that instead cites, e.g., pattern completion/denoising or multistable choice as the two computations should still be checked for the same standard: does the explanation correctly identify superposition or homogeneity as the violated property, and is the mechanism explained (not just "linear networks can't do nonlinear things")?
