**Grader key** (teacher AI only — verify each point against you's physical copy of D&A ch. 9 before marking; do not invent page numbers or quotations; the text below is expected content to check for, not a substitute for the source)

1. Expected: RL is driven by a scalar evaluative ("how good was that") signal from the environment, not a target output (supervised) or input statistics alone (unsupervised). The signal says how good the outcome was but never says what the correct action *would have been* — no error vector pointing toward a known right answer, only a scalar reward.
2. Expected content: acquisition = CS paired with US repeatedly, learned association strengthens over trials. Extinction = CS repeatedly presented without US after conditioning, learned association weakens. Blocking = CS-A conditioned to US alone first; when compound CS-AB is then paired with the same US, CS-B acquires little or no association despite being paired with the US just as often as in an unblocked control. Overshadowing = compound AB trained from the start (no pretraining); the more salient stimulus acquires more associative strength than the other, even though both are paired with US equally. Secondary conditioning = a CS that itself has been conditioned to predict reward can act like a US for a further, second CS chained to it.
3. Expected: blocking rules out contiguity because CS-B co-occurs with the US exactly as often as CS-A did during compound training, yet B acquires little strength — mere temporal pairing cannot be the whole story, since B is paired with the US just as reliably as A was and still fails to condition. The rule must instead be sensitive to whether the US is already *predicted* by other stimuli present on the trial — i.e., error/surprise, not raw co-occurrence.
4. Expected: prediction error = actual reward minus the reward currently predicted. D&A write the RW rule with the prediction as the *sum* of the associative strengths of all stimuli present on that trial (v_total = v_A + v_B + ...), and the single scalar error δ = r − v_total is used to update every present stimulus's strength. This summed-prediction form is exactly what produces blocking: if v_A alone already equals r, δ = 0 and v_B never grows.
5. Expected: exploration = trying actions whose value is uncertain to learn about them; exploitation = repeatedly choosing the currently best-known action to reap reward. Softmax action selection assigns probability proportional to exp(β·value) for each action; at low β (high "temperature") choices are nearly uniform/random (exploration-heavy), at high β the policy collapses toward always picking the max-value action (exploitation-heavy). β (or its inverse) is the parameter that tunes this balance.
6. Expected: the indirect actor learns action *values* (an estimate of expected reward per action) via a reward-prediction-error rule, then derives its policy from those values via a separate decision rule (e.g. softmax). It is "indirect" with respect to the policy: reward shapes the policy only by first shaping a value estimate, which the policy then reads off; nothing directly reinforces the chosen action's probability.
7. Expected: the critic performs policy evaluation — it learns V(s), an estimate of value under the current policy. The actor performs policy improvement — it adjusts the parameters governing action selection so as to choose actions the critic judges favorably.
8. Expected: bootstrapping means the update for V(s_t) uses the critic's *own current estimate* of V(s_{t+1}) as a stand-in for all reward expected after t+1, rather than waiting to observe the full remaining reward sequence. This lets V be updated on every step, before the trial/episode ends, and lets information about reward propagate backward through the value function one step at a time as estimates improve.

## Piece 1 — Four formal machines, worked by hand

**Type:** derivation · **Time:** 90-120 min

**Learning goals**
- Derive Rescorla-Wagner trial dynamics from raw numbers, not from the closed-form solution.
- Show mechanically, with numbers, which term in the update produces blocking.
- Derive (not assert) which within-trial timesteps carry nonzero TD error, before and after convergence.
- State the critic and actor update equations and identify exactly what quantity couples them.

**Task**

**Part A — Rescorla-Wagner acquisition (AC1).** A single CS is paired with a US of reward magnitude r = 1 every trial. Initial associative strength V₀ = 0, learning rate α = 0.2. The Rescorla-Wagner rule is:
δₙ = r − Vₙ (prediction error on trial n, computed *before* the update)
Vₙ₊₁ = Vₙ + α·δₙ
By hand, fill in V (before), δ, and V (after) for trials 1-4.

**Part B — Blocking (AC2).** Stimulus A is trained alone for 4 trials under the same rule (α = 0.2, r = 1) — reuse Part A's numbers, so V_A after 4 trials is known. Then compound AB training begins: V_B starts at 0, and on every AB trial the *shared* prediction error is computed from the sum:
δₙ = r − (V_A,ₙ + V_B,ₙ)
ΔV_A = ΔV_B = α·δₙ (both present stimuli get the identical increment, since both are present and equally salient)
By hand, compute V_A, V_B, and δ for AB trials 1-4 (i.e., trials 5-8 overall). Then answer: what stays constant across every AB trial, and why does that quantity determine how much blocking occurs? What would V_A and V_B converge to if A had instead been pretrained for 30 trials before AB began (state the limiting logic; you do not need to hand-compute 30 trials)?

**Part C — TD error within a trial (AC3).** A trial has six timesteps, t = 0 (cue onset) through t = 5 (reward delivery, r = 1; r = 0 at all other t). Value is represented as one weight per timestep, wₜ = V(t), all initialized to 0. Terminal value V(6) = 0 (end of trial). Learning rate α = 0.3, discount γ = 1. The TD rule, applied at every timestep within every trial:
δₜ = rₜ + γ·V(t+1) − V(t)
V(t) ← V(t) + α·δₜ (only the active timestep's weight updates)
By hand, compute δ₀...δ₅ and the resulting weights after trial 1, then after trial 2. State in words what happens to the *location* of the nonzero error as trials 1, 2, 3, ... proceed, and state what δ at t = 5 equals once V has converged (give the exact converged weights, not just "it's zero because it's zero" — show why).

**Part D — Actor-critic and the indirect actor (AC4, AC5).**
(i) Write the critic's update equation for V(sₜ) in an actor-critic architecture, and state which single quantity from that equation is also handed to the actor.
(ii) Indirect actor: for a two-action choice with action values m₁ = m₂ = 0, action 1 is chosen and reward r = 1 is received. Using update m_a ← m_a + α(r − m_a) with α = 0.3, compute m₁ after the trial. Using softmax P(a) = exp(β·m_a)/Σ_b exp(β·m_b) with β = 2, compute the resulting P(a₁) and P(a₂) (to 4 decimal places).
(iii) Direct actor: for the same trial, using a direct probability update p_a ← p_a + α·r·(1 − p_a) for the chosen action and p_b ← p_b − α·r·p_b for the other, with initial p₁ = p₂ = 0.5 and α = 0.3, compute p₁ and p₂ after the trial. Confirm p₁ + p₂ = 1 still holds and explain in one sentence why this update needs no separate softmax step while the indirect actor's does.

The invariant is V_A − V_B = 0.5904 (the gap that existed the instant AB training began), because every AB trial adds the *identical* increment α·δ to both A and B (same shared δ, same α). The pair converges toward V_A+V_B = 1 while holding V_A − V_B fixed, giving V_A → (1+0.5904)/2 = 0.7952, V_B → (1−0.5904)/2 = 0.2048 (partial blocking — B is suppressed but not zero, because A hadn't reached full asymptote).
Limiting case, A pretrained near-fully (e.g. 30 trials, V_A ≈ 0.998762 — confirmed by running the rule): the initial gap ≈ 0.998762, so V_B → (1 − 0.998762)/2 ≈ 0.00062 — full blocking in the limit A → 1. Confirmed numerically: after 30 A-alone trials then 20 AB trials, V_A = 0.999381, V_B = 0.000619. The controlling term is the *shared, summed* prediction error δ = r − (V_A+V_B): because δ is already ≈ 0 once A alone accounts for nearly all of r, there is almost no error left over to teach B anything.

*Part C* (α = 0.3, γ = 1, cue at t=0, reward r=1 at t=5, 6 timesteps 0-5, V(6)=0 terminal):

Trial 1: δ = [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 1.000], w after = [0, 0, 0, 0, 0, 0.300].
Trial 2: δ = [0, 0, 0, 0, 0.300, 0.700], w after = [0, 0, 0, 0, 0.090, 0.510].
Trial 3: δ = [0, 0, 0, 0.090, 0.420, 0.490], w after = [0, 0, 0, 0.027, 0.216, 0.657].
Trial 4: δ = [0, 0, 0.027, 0.189, 0.441, 0.343], w after = [0, 0, 0.0081, 0.0837, 0.3483, 0.7599].

Pattern (confirmed by running 200 trials): the timestep of *first* nonzero δ moves one step earlier every trial — trial 1 nonzero only at t=5; trial 2 nonzero at t=4,5; trial 3 nonzero at t=3,4,5; and so on — because δₜ = rₜ + γ·w_{t+1} − wₜ is only nonzero at t if w_{t+1} already carries value that wₜ has not yet caught up to, and that "already carries value" property itself moves backward one step per trial. By trial 20, δ is spread and shrinking across t=0..4 (e.g. [0.192, 0.149, 0.087, 0.036, 0.009, 0.001]); by trial ~150, all δ ≈ 0 to machine precision. Converged weights: w = [1, 1, 1, 1, 1, 1] (every timestep predicts the full eventual reward of 1, since reward is deterministic). At convergence, δ at t=5 = r + γ·V(6) − V(5) = 1 + 1·0 − 1 = 0 exactly — not because "nothing happens" but because V(5) already equals the reward it's about to receive.
Reward-omission probe after convergence (r=0 at t=5 instead of 1): δ = [0,0,0,0,0,−1.000] — a dip exactly to −1 at the expected reward time, since δ₅ = 0 + 0 − V(5) = −1.

*Part D*: (i) Critic: V(sₜ) ← V(sₜ) + α_c·δₜ, δₜ = rₜ + γ·V(s_{t+1}) − V(sₜ). The quantity handed to the actor is δₜ itself (the TD error), used to update the action preference for the action taken: e.g. p(sₜ,aₜ) ← p(sₜ,aₜ) + α_a·δₜ.
(ii) m₁ ← 0 + 0.3(1−0) = 0.300, m₂ = 0. P(a₁) = e^{0.6}/(e^{0.6}+e^0) = 1.8221/(1.8221+1) = 1.8221/2.8221 = 0.6459. P(a₂) = 1/2.8221 = 0.3541.
(iii) p₁ ← 0.5 + 0.3·1·(1−0.5) = 0.5+0.15 = 0.650. p₂ ← 0.5 − 0.3·1·0.5 = 0.5−0.15 = 0.350. Sum = 1.000 ✓. No softmax step is needed because the direct actor's update already operates on probabilities and preserves normalization by construction (what's added to the chosen action's probability is subtracted, in total, from the others); the indirect actor's m_a is not a probability at all — it needs the exponential/normalization step to become one.

**Grader key** (teacher AI only — this is a synthesis piece; there is no single correct text, but every strong answer must contain the following claims, stated correctly)

(a) Supervised learning receives a target/label that directly says what the correct output was, giving a full error *vector*/gradient direction; unsupervised learning uses input statistics alone with no evaluative signal; RL's reward is a single scalar saying how good an outcome was, with no information about what a better action would have been — you must discover that by trial and error, which is why exploration is necessary in the action-choice setting (Piece 3, Q5) but not in supervised learning.

(b) RW's error δ = r − ΣV(stimuli present) is computed once per trial from the *total* predicted reward. Ordinary acquisition: with one stimulus, this reduces to the single-stimulus case, δ shrinks geometrically as V→r (Piece 1 Part A). Blocking: when A is already near-asymptote, the sum V_A+V_B is already ≈ r before B has ever been reinforced, so δ ≈ 0 from B's first paired trial onward, and B's update α·δ is correspondingly ≈0 (Piece 1/2 Part B). If each stimulus instead had its own independent error δ_A = r − V_A, δ_B = r − V_B, then B's error would be r − 0 = r every trial regardless of A, and B would acquire full strength just as if trained alone — no blocking would occur. The sum is not a detail; it is the entire mechanism.

(c) TD represents value at each within-trial timestep and updates V(t) using δₜ = rₜ + γV(t+1) − V(t) — using the *estimate* of future value at t+1 rather than waiting for the trial to finish. Before learning, only the reward timestep has nonzero error (nothing predicts anything yet). After one trial, the timestep just before reward inherits a nonzero error too, because it can now see (via V(t+1)) that value exists one step ahead that it hasn't yet accounted for. This repeats: each trial, the earliest timestep whose successor "knows something it doesn't yet" is exactly one step earlier than the previous trial's earliest such timestep — producing the observed one-step-per-trial backward shift, not as a separate assumption but as a direct consequence of bootstrapping off V(t+1).

(d) In actor-critic, the critic computes exactly this TD error δₜ to update its own V(s) (policy evaluation). The actor receives the same δₜ, not a separately computed signal, and uses it to adjust the preference for whichever action was taken at that state (policy improvement) — an action that led to a better-than-expected outcome (δ>0) has its preference increased, worse-than-expected (δ<0) decreased. The critic's bootstrapped error is therefore doing double duty: it is both the critic's own learning signal and the only information the actor receives about how well its last choice went.
