## A note on parameters before you start

F5 worked in Hodgkin-Huxley's numbers — a squid giant axon at 6.3 °C, where the membrane time constant is about 3.3 ms. This lesson uses tau_m = 10 ms, which is a mammalian cortical neuron at body temperature. Neither is wrong and neither is a typo: they are different preparations, and the integrate-and-fire parameters here are chosen to match the cells the rest of the course models. If a number from F5 does not reproduce here, check the preparation before you check your arithmetic.

## Piece 1 — LIF derivation, f-I curve, and shunting inhibition

**Type:** derivation · **Time:** 70–90 min

**Learning goals**
- Derive the LIF equation as a reduction of the F5 single-compartment membrane equation, with tau_m, threshold, and reset as explicit parameters.
- Solve the subthreshold LIF equation and use it to derive the interspike interval (ISI) and firing rate as functions of injected current.
- Add a refractory period and find the current at which it starts to reshape the f-I curve, with a numeric criterion.
- Derive, from the membrane equation with a conductance-based synaptic term, why a conductance synapse changes the effective membrane time constant even when it carries no net current at rest — and name the effect.

**Task**

*Task A — LIF and its f-I curve.*
1. Start from the single-compartment membrane equation from F5: `C dV/dt = -g_L(V - E_L) + I_e(t)`. Divide through by `g_L` and define `tau_m = C/g_L = R C` (R = 1/g_L) to get the standard LIF form: `tau_m dV/dt = -(V - V_rest) + R I_e`, with `V_rest = E_L`. State in one sentence why this is a reduction, not a new model: what part of the F5 equation did you just delete?
2. Solve this linear ODE for constant `I_e`, starting from `V(0) = V_reset`. Show the general subthreshold solution `V(t)`, and identify the steady-state (asymptotic) voltage `V_ss = V_rest + R I_e`.
3. Using parameters `V_rest = -65 mV`, `V_threshold = -50 mV`, `V_reset = -70 mV`, `tau_m = 10 ms`, `R = 10 MΩ`, and `I_e = 2 nA`: compute `V_ss`, then solve for the time `t_isi` at which `V(t)` first reaches `V_threshold`. Give `t_isi` in ms and the corresponding firing rate `1/t_isi` in Hz.
4. Add an absolute refractory period `t_ref = 2 ms` (after each spike, `V` is clamped to `V_reset` for `t_ref` before the subthreshold equation resumes). Give the new interspike interval and firing rate.
5. Find the injected current `I_e` at which `t_ref` equals `t_isi` (i.e., the refractory period accounts for exactly half the total ISI at that point). Show your algebra symbolically before plugging in numbers. State in one sentence what happens to the f-I curve's shape above this current, and what value the firing rate approaches as `I_e → ∞`.

*Task B — conductance-based synapse and shunting inhibition.*
6. Extend the membrane equation with one synaptic conductance: `C dV/dt = -g_L(V - E_L) - g_syn(t)(V - E_syn) + I_e`. Collect terms in `V` and show that this is still a linear ODE in `V`, with an effective time constant `tau_eff = C / (g_L + g_syn)` and a new effective resting point. Do this algebraically — do not just state the result.
7. Now set `E_syn = V_rest` (an inhibitory synapse reversing right at rest, e.g. GABA-A near `-65 mV`) and evaluate the synaptic current `-g_syn(V - E_syn)` at `V = V_rest`. What is its numeric value, for any `g_syn`? Given that answer, explain how `tau_eff` can still be shorter than `tau_m` — walk through the algebra of `tau_eff` versus `tau_m` explicitly, don't just assert it changes.
8. Using `C = 100 pF`, `g_L = 10 nS` (so `tau_m = 10 ms`), and an inhibitory conductance `g_syn = 10 nS` with `E_syn = V_rest`: compute `tau_eff` numerically. Name the phenomenon this demonstrates.

**Deliverable**

Handwritten or typed pen-and-paper derivation for Tasks A and B, in order, showing every algebraic step (not just final formulas) and every substitution of numbers with units carried through. Final numeric answers stated on their own line, e.g. `t_isi = 16.09 ms`.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | All 8 steps present with correct algebra and correct final numbers (within rounding); Task A step 5 shown symbolically before the numeric current is computed; Task B step 7 explicitly evaluates the driving force at rest before arguing about `tau_eff`, not just asserting shunting occurs. |
| Adequate | LIF reduction, subthreshold solution, and Task A criteria 1–2 numbers correct; refractory-dominance current attempted with correct method even if arithmetic slips; Task B derives `tau_eff = C/(g_L+g_syn)` correctly but the "zero net current, shorter tau anyway" argument is asserted rather than derived from the algebra. |
| Not yet | Formulas copied without derivation steps; numeric answers wrong or missing units; Task B treats shunting as "inhibition that also lowers V" rather than a time-constant effect. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only)

- LIF form: `tau_m dV/dt = -(V - V_rest) + R I_e`, `tau_m = RC`. The deleted term is the entire spike-generating machinery (`g_Na m^3 h (V-E_Na)`, `g_K n^4(V-E_K)` and their gating kinetics) — the LIF keeps only the passive leak term and adds a threshold-and-reset rule by hand.
- Subthreshold solution: `V(t) = V_ss + (V_reset - V_ss) e^{-t/tau_m}`, `V_ss = V_rest + R I_e`.
- At `I_e = 2 nA`: `R I_e = 10e6 Ω × 2e-9 A = 0.02 V = 20 mV`. `V_ss = -65 + 20 = -45 mV`.
  `t_isi = tau_m ln[(V_ss - V_reset)/(V_ss - V_threshold)] = 10 ms × ln[(-45-(-70))/(-45-(-50))] = 10 ms × ln(25/5) = 10 ms × ln 5 = 16.09 ms`.
  Rate `= 1/16.09 ms = 62.1 Hz`.
- With `t_ref = 2 ms`: ISI `= 16.09 + 2 = 18.09 ms`, rate `= 1000/18.09 = 55.3 Hz`. (A numerical Euler simulation at dt=0.01–0.001 ms reproduces this to within ~1 Hz — 55.0 Hz measured — the small gap is discretization, not a derivation error.)
- Dominance current: set `t_isi = t_ref`. With `r = e^{t_ref/tau_m} = e^{0.2} = 1.2214`: `V_ss = (V_reset - r·V_threshold)/(1-r) = (-70 - 1.2214×(-50)) / (1-1.2214) = (-70+61.07)/(-0.2214) = -8.93/-0.2214 = 40.33 mV`.
  `R I_e = V_ss - V_rest = 40.33 - (-65) = 105.33 mV`, so `I_e = 0.10533 V / 10 MΩ = 10.53 nA`.
  Above this current the refractory period is more than half the ISI and the curve flattens; as `I_e → ∞`, rate → `1/t_ref = 1/2ms = 500 Hz` (a hard ceiling the LIF never exceeds). Simulation check: 250 Hz measured at 10.5 nA (matches the predicted 50%-refractory point exactly), 330 Hz at 20 nA still climbing toward 500 Hz.
- Task B step 6: expand `-g_L(V-E_L) - g_syn(V-E_syn) = -(g_L+g_syn)V + (g_L E_L + g_syn E_syn)`. Dividing by `C`: `dV/dt = -(V - V_eff)/tau_eff + I_e/C`, with `tau_eff = C/(g_L+g_syn)` and `V_eff = (g_L E_L + g_syn E_syn)/(g_L+g_syn)`.
- Step 7: at `V=V_rest=E_syn`, `-g_syn(V-E_syn) = -g_syn×0 = 0` for *any* `g_syn` — no net current. But `tau_eff = C/(g_L+g_syn)` depends on the sum of conductances regardless of driving force, so it shrinks whenever `g_syn>0` even though the current term vanished at that instant.
- Step 8: `tau_eff = 100 pF / (10+10) nS = 100/20 = 5 ms` (half of `tau_m = 10 ms`). Phenomenon: **shunting inhibition** — the effect acts through conductance (hence effective time constant), not through delivered current.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: Point them back to the F5 membrane equation and ask which terms are HH-specific vs. passive-leak-specific.
- Hint 2: If stuck on the ISI algebra, ask them to write the subthreshold solution first and solve `V(t_isi) = V_threshold` for `t_isi` symbolically, substituting numbers only at the end.
- Hint 3: If stuck on the dominance current, ask what condition on `t_isi` and `t_ref` defines "the refractory period accounts for half the ISI," and have them set up that equation before touching numbers.
- Never: give the numeric answer to steps 3–5 or 7–8 directly. Never derive the shunting argument for them — if they assert "conductance change shifts V and that's inhibition," push back and ask them to evaluate the current term at `V=V_rest` explicitly.

## Piece 2 — Simulation: LIF vs. HH f-I curves, and shunting made visible

**Type:** simulation · **Time:** 60–75 min

**Learning goals**
- Run LIF and Hodgkin-Huxley side by side on the same injected-current sweep and read off where the LIF abstraction holds and where it breaks.
- Observe a concrete case of a phenomenon LIF cannot produce, as a simulation result rather than a memorized fact.
- Watch shunting inhibition directly: a conductance-based inhibitory input that delivers ~zero net current at rest but still visibly shrinks and speeds up an EPSP, compared with a current-based synapse that does nothing when matched to zero current at rest.

**Task**

Write and run a self-contained Python script (numpy only, no downloads) with two parts.

*Part A.* Implement `hh_rate(I)`: standard Hodgkin-Huxley (`C=1 µF/cm²`, `g_Na=120`, `g_K=36`, `g_L=0.3 mS/cm²`, `E_Na=50`, `E_K=-77`, `E_L=-54.4 mV`, standard alpha/beta gating kinetics) with a constant injected current density `I` (µA/cm²), Euler-integrated at `dt=0.01 ms` over 300 ms, discarding the first 100 ms as settling time, counting upward threshold crossings through `V=0 mV`. Implement `lif_rate(I)`: the LIF from Piece 1 (same parameters, `t_ref=2 ms`), same integration and counting scheme, with `I` in nA. Sweep both across a current range that spans each model's rheobase up to several times over, and print firing rate vs. current for each. Identify: (a) the current near which each model's firing rate turns on from zero, and whether that onset is gradual or abrupt; (b) whether either model's rate ever drops back toward zero at high current, and if so, why (inspect the gating variables, not just the voltage, at that current).

*Part B.* Implement a passive (non-spiking) neuron (`C=100 pF`, `g_L=10 nS`, `V_rest=-65 mV`) receiving an AMPA-like excitatory conductance pulse (`E_exc=0 mV`, alpha-function shape, peak `5 nS`, `tau=2 ms`, onset `t=50 ms`) and, in a separate condition, a concurrent inhibitory conductance step (`g_inh=50 nS`, `E_inh=-65 mV` — exactly at rest — active from `t=45` to `80 ms`). Run three conditions: no inhibition, conductance-based inhibition as above, and the "matched" current-based inhibition (a constant current equal to `g_inh × (E_inh − V_rest)`, computed once at rest). Measure and report the EPSP peak (mV above rest) in each condition.

**Deliverable**

The script (< 100 lines), its printed output, and 4–6 sentences stating: the LIF and HH onset behavior (gradual vs. abrupt, with the currents), what happens to the HH rate at high current and which gating variable is responsible, and the three EPSP peak values from Part B with a one-line explanation of why the current-based condition differs from the conductance-based one.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | Both models implemented correctly and swept over a broad enough range to see HH's rheobase and its high-current rollback; the HH rollback is diagnosed by inspecting a gating variable (not just "rate went to zero"); Part B reports three distinct, correctly computed EPSP peaks with the current-based case identified as ~equal to baseline. |
| Adequate | f-I curves computed and roughly match expected shapes; high-current HH behavior noted but not diagnosed mechanistically; Part B run with at least the no-inhibition and conductance-inhibition conditions compared correctly. |
| Not yet | Only one model implemented, or integration is unstable (NaN/blow-up) without being caught and fixed; Part B skipped or the current-based "matched" condition not computed from `g_inh × (E_inh − V_rest)`. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only — from an actual run, Euler `dt=0.01 ms`; exact numbers will vary a few Hz / a few % with integrator/settling choices, expect this and check for the right qualitative story, not exact digit matches)

- HH f-I: silent for `I ≲ 6.2 µA/cm²`; between `I=6.2` and `I=6.3 µA/cm²` the rate jumps discontinuously from 0 Hz to about 50–55 Hz (not a gradual rise from 0) — this is the abrupt ("class 2"-like) onset. Rate then rises further with current (≈100 Hz by 30 µA/cm², ≈120–125 Hz by 60 µA/cm²).
- HH at high current: between `I=60` and `I=65 µA/cm²` the firing rate collapses from ≈123 Hz to 0 Hz and stays at 0 through `I=100 µA/cm²`. Checking the trace at `I=100 µA/cm²`: `V` settles to a depolarized plateau around `-38 mV` (not resting, not spiking), with `h ≈ 0.12` (Na⁺ inactivation variable collapsed) and `n ≈ 0.59`. This is **depolarization block**: persistent Na⁺ inactivation stops spike generation even though the neuron is being driven hard. It is not a numerical blow-up (voltage stays bounded and physiological).
- LIF f-I: rheobase exactly `1.5 nA` (analytic, `R·I_min = V_th - V_rest = 15 mV`); rate rises continuously and monotonically from 0 Hz just above rheobase (≈30 Hz at 1.6 nA measured) with no collapse, reaching ≈250 Hz at 10.5 nA and ≈330 Hz at 20 nA, still climbing toward the 500 Hz ceiling (`1/t_ref`) derived in Piece 1. LIF never depolarization-blocks because it has no inactivation variable to collapse.
- Named phenomenon for criterion 3: **depolarization block** — LIF cannot produce it because it discards Na⁺ channel inactivation (the HH `h` gating variable), replacing the entire spike-and-recovery cycle with an instantaneous fixed threshold-and-reset rule.
- Part B EPSP peaks (measured, `dt=0.01 ms`): no inhibition ≈ `9.55 mV` above rest; conductance-based inhibition ≈ `4.01 mV` above rest (≈58% smaller); current-based "matched" inhibition: `I = g_inh × (E_inh - V_rest) = 50 nS × (-65 - (-65)) mV = 0 nA` exactly, so this condition is numerically identical to the no-inhibition trace (`9.55 mV`). Independently, a direct step-decay test gives `tau_m ≈ 10.0 ms` with no inhibition and `tau_eff ≈ 1.66–1.67 ms` with `g_inh=50 nS` active, matching the Piece 1 prediction `C/(g_L+g_inh) = 100/(10+50) = 1.667 ms` almost exactly.
- The punchline the learner should state: the conductance-based synapse delivers zero net current at the instant it matters most (rest) yet cuts the EPSP by more than half via `tau_eff`; the "equivalent" current-based synapse, matched the only way that makes sense at rest (zero net current), does *nothing at all*. This is the demonstration that the current-vs-conductance distinction is not cosmetic.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: If the HH integration blows up or goes to NaN, suggest checking the `alpha_n`/`alpha_m` singularities at `V=-55` and `V=-40 mV` (0/0 limits) and handling them with a small-`x` fallback rather than dividing by zero.
- Hint 2: If they can't find where HH's rate goes to zero at high current, tell them to widen the current sweep past 60 µA/cm² and to plot or print `h` alongside `V`.
- Hint 3: If Part B's current-based condition confuses them (they expect a nonzero effect), ask them to compute `g_inh × (E_inh - V_rest)` by hand before running it.
- Never: hand them a working HH implementation. Never tell them the exact onset or collapse currents before they've run their own sweep — let the numbers in their output drive the observation.

## Piece 3 — Guided reading: D&A ch. 5

**Learning goals**
- Locate, in the text, what the integrate-and-fire reduction is explicitly said to give up relative to conductance-based (HH-type) models.
- Extract the standard synaptic conductance time courses and reversal potentials the text gives for AMPA, NMDA, and GABA-A.

**Task**

1. What does *Integrate-and-Fire Models* say the model omits relative to a full conductance-based (Hodgkin-Huxley-style) description? List at least two specific omissions the text names (not your own guesses).

5. In *Synapses on Integrate-and-Fire Neurons*, how does the text define the total synaptic conductance driving the neuron, and how is it combined with the leak conductance in the membrane equation?

**Rubric**

| Adequate | 4 of 5 answered correctly; Q4 has values for at least two of the three synapse types; answers are traceable to the right sections even if brief. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only — expected content; the physical text is the only source, verify the learner's answer against it directly before marking, since this teacher AI cannot read the book)

- Q1/Q2 (expected direction, not exact wording): the IF reduction discards the spike-generating conductance dynamics themselves — no explicit sodium/potassium kinetics, no real spike waveform, no built-in refractoriness (it must be added as an extra rule), and no mechanism for spike-frequency adaptation or bursting, which in real/HH-extended neurons arise from additional slow currents (e.g., Ca²⁺-activated K⁺ current for adaptation, T-type Ca²⁺ current for bursting) that a plain leaky-integrator has no room for.
- Q3 (expected direction): the text models a synaptic conductance's time course as a rise-and-decay waveform — commonly a single exponential decay from an instantaneous jump, or an alpha function `g(t) ∝ (t/tau) exp(1-t/tau)`, parameterized by a peak conductance and a time constant (or separate rise/decay time constants for a difference-of-exponentials form). Verify which specific form D&A uses against the book — do not accept the learner's answer on faith if it names a form without a parameter.
- Q4 (expected, standard literature values — confirm the learner's cited numbers against the book's actual table/text before grading strictly): AMPA fast, decay on the order of ~1–2 ms, reversal near 0 mV; NMDA slow, decay on the order of tens to ~100+ ms (also voltage-dependent via Mg²⁺ block), reversal near 0 mV; GABA-A intermediate, decay on the order of ~5–20 ms, reversal near −70 mV (chloride equilibrium, close to typical rest). Order by decay: AMPA fastest, GABA-A intermediate, NMDA slowest. Accept the learner's numbers as correct if they are in the right order and roughly this order of magnitude and they cite the book's actual figures — the teacher AI must check the physical text, not assume these placeholder ranges are exactly what D&A prints.
- Q5 (expected direction): total synaptic conductance is a sum over active synapses, each contributing `g_syn,i(t)` with its own reversal potential; the membrane equation becomes `C dV/dt = -g_L(V-E_L) - Σ_i g_syn,i(t)(V - E_syn,i) + I_e`, i.e., synaptic conductances add onto the leak conductance term rather than acting as independent injected currents — this is the same structure the learner derived in Piece 1 Task B.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: If they can't find the omissions list, point them to the paragraph in *Integrate-and-Fire Models* that motivates why the model is used despite being simpler than HH.
- Hint 2: If Q4 values are missing, point them to any table or worked example in *Synaptic Conductances* rather than giving the numbers directly.
- Hint 3: If Q5 is vague, ask them to write the full membrane equation with a sum over synapses, mirroring what they built in Piece 1 Task B.
- Never: supply page numbers, quotations, or specific numeric values from the book — you (the teacher) do not have access to the text and must not fabricate its contents. Ask the learner to state what page/section they found each answer on, and treat any teacher-side "expected" values above as a sanity check only, not ground truth to overrule the book.
