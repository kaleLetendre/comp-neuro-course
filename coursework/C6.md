# C6 Coursework — PC ≈ backprop: what the equivalence does and does not claim

**Lesson spec:** `lessons.md` → C6. **Total time:** ~5.5 hours across two sittings (sitting 1: Pieces 1–2, ~3h; sitting 2: Pieces 3–4, ~2.5h). **Prerequisites:** C1–C5; standard backprop; the spiking credit-assignment problem.

## Running order

Consume, then apply, then refine:

**audio overview → guided reading (Piece 3) → derivation (Piece 1) → simulation (Piece 2) → written defence (Piece 4)**

Pieces are numbered by type, not by sequence. The reading front-loads the concepts, the derivation makes them exact, the simulation is where they stick and get refined, and the defence is where you find out whether they did.

## Verified against the source

Checked against both C6 papers. Two corrections to how they are described:

**The name is later terminology.** Whittington & Bogacz (2017) do not use the phrase "fixed-prediction assumption"; that label comes from subsequent literature, including the Millidge review. Their paper sets out the conditions in its own terms. Do not mark a learner wrong for failing to find the phrase in the text, and do not accept a claim that the paper uses it.

**Z-IL's timing condition is concrete.** Song et al. specify weight updates at particular **inference moments** during the inference phase rather than after convergence, which is what makes the correspondence exact rather than approximate. The paper also distinguishes Z-IL from a fully autonomous variant (Fa-Z-IL) that removes the external control signal. A strong answer to the timing question names the per-moment schedule, not merely "update earlier".

## Piece 1 — Fixed-Prediction Derivation: One Weight, Two Rules

**Type:** derivation · **Time:** 75 min

**Learning goals**
- Compute a backprop gradient and a PC weight update on the same tiny network from first principles.
- Locate exactly where the fixed-prediction assumption enters the PC inference dynamics.
- Show numerically that the two rules coincide in the small-step limit and diverge once inference is allowed to relax further.

**Task**

Use this fixed network (no need to reproduce it from a paper — it is small enough to hand-derive):

- 1 input, 1 hidden unit, 1 output unit, all scalar.
- Weights: `w1 = 0.5` (input → hidden), `w2 = 0.8` (hidden → output). No biases.
- Nonlinearity: `tanh` on both hidden and output units.
- Input `x0 = 1.0`, target `t = 1.0`, squared-error loss `L = 0.5(t - o)^2`.

**Part A — backprop.** Do the forward pass by hand (compute `h`, `o`, `L`). Do the backward pass by hand: compute `δ_o`, `∂L/∂w2`, `δ_h`, `∂L/∂w1`.

**Part B — PC, fixed-prediction regime.** Set up the PC network with the same connectivity: value nodes `v0 = x0` (clamped), `v1` (hidden), `v2` (clamped to `t`); predictions `pred1 = f(w1 v0)`, `pred2 = f(w2 v1)`; error nodes `ε1 = v1 - pred1`, `ε2 = v2 - pred2`. Initialize `v1` at its feedforward value (`v1(0) = h`), so `ε1(0) = 0`. Write the inference gradient `dv1/dτ = -ε1 + ε2 · f'(w2 v1) · w2` and evaluate it at `τ = 0`. State in your own words what "fixed prediction" is doing here — which term is being held at its start-of-inference value rather than re-differentiated. Derive the weight-update rule `Δw1 ∝ ε1 · f'(w1 v0) · v0` at first order in a small inference step, and show it matches `∂L/∂w1` from Part A.

**Part C — relax the assumption.** Now run inference for two full (not infinitesimal) Euler steps of size 1 instead of stopping at the first-order limit. Recompute `ε1` after the second step and the resulting `Δw1`. Compare this value to the backprop gradient from Part A as a percentage divergence.

**Deliverable**

A single page (handwritten photo, scanned PDF, or typed) with: Part A's four numbers, Part B's derivation and the matched value, Part C's two-step recomputation and a stated percentage divergence, and 2–3 sentences naming which term (`ε1` starting at exactly zero, versus growing over further inference steps) causes the divergence.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | Parts A and B numerically match to the precision worked (backprop `∂L/∂w1` and the fixed-prediction PC update agree to within rounding); Part C shows a real divergence (order tens of percent) with the arithmetic shown, not asserted; the 2–3 sentences correctly identify that `ε1(0)=0` by construction is what forces the match, and that letting `v1` move away from the feedforward value is what breaks it. |
| Adequate | Part A is correct; Part B is attempted with the right structure (identifies `ε` and predictions correctly) but has an arithmetic slip; Part C is attempted and shows *some* divergence even if the number is off, with a plausible qualitative explanation. |
| Not yet | Backprop arithmetic wrong, or Part B skips straight to "they're equal" without deriving `dv1/dτ` and the update rule, or Part C is omitted or just re-states Part B's numbers without actually running further inference steps. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only)

Reference computation (values to 4 dp; accept learner's numbers within ±0.01 absolute or ±5% relative — small tanh-approximation differences are expected):

- Forward: `h = tanh(0.5) = 0.4621`, `o_pre = 0.8·0.4621 = 0.3697`, `o = tanh(0.3697) ≈ 0.3536`, `L ≈ 0.2085`.
- Backprop: `δ_o = -(t-o)(1-o²) = -(0.6464)(0.8750) = -0.5656`; `∂L/∂w2 = δ_o·h = -0.2615`. `dL/dh = δ_o·w2 = -0.4525`; `δ_h = dL/dh·(1-h²) = -0.4525·0.7865 = -0.3559`; `∂L/∂w1 = δ_h·x0 = -0.3559`.
- PC top layer (always exact, no assumption needed): `ε2(0) = t - o = 0.6464` (this is exactly `-∂L/∂o`). `Δw2 ∝ ε2·(1-o²)·h = 0.6464·0.8750·0.4621 = 0.2615` — matches `-∂L/∂w2` exactly. This is *not* the interesting layer; the fixed-prediction assumption is invisible at the layer touching the clamped output.
- PC hidden layer, fixed-prediction (Part B): `dv1/dτ|₀ = -ε1(0) + ε2(0)·(1-o²)·w2 = 0 + 0.6464·0.8750·0.8 = 0.4525`, which equals `-dL/dh` exactly. First-order weight-update coefficient: `0.4525·(1-h²) = 0.4525·0.7865 = 0.3559` — matches `-∂L/∂w1` exactly. **This is the whole content of the fixed-prediction assumption**: at `τ=0`, `ε1=0` by construction (v1 was initialized at the feedforward value), so the `-ε1` term in the inference ODE drops out, leaving exactly the backprop-shaped term.
- PC hidden layer, relaxed (Part C), two unit Euler steps: step 1 gives `v1(1) = 0.4621 + 0.4525 = 0.9146`, `ε1(1) = 0.9146 - 0.4621 = 0.4525`. Step 2: `pred2(v1=0.9146) = tanh(0.7317) ≈ 0.6239`, `ε2(1) = 0.3761`, `(1-pred2²) = 0.6107`, `dv1/dτ|₁ = -0.4525 + 0.3761·0.6107·0.8 = -0.4525 + 0.1837 = -0.2688`. `v1(2) = 0.9146 - 0.2688 = 0.6458`, `ε1(2) = 0.6458 - 0.4621 = 0.1837`. Weight-update coefficient: `0.1837·0.7865 = 0.1445`.
- Divergence: `0.1445` vs. backprop's `0.3559` → PC retains only ~41% of the backprop-matching magnitude, i.e. **~59% divergence** once inference is allowed to run past the fixed-prediction (`τ→0`) point. Accept anything in the ballpark of "several tens of percent off, and growing with more/larger inference steps" — the exact percentage is integration-scheme-dependent (Euler step size, number of steps), so do not fail a learner for landing at 40% or 70% if their steps are shown and internally consistent.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: "What is `ε1` equal to at the very first instant of inference, given how `v1` was initialized? Why?"
- Hint 2: "Write out `dv1/dτ` as a sum of two terms. Which one vanishes at `τ=0`, and why does that make the remaining term look like a backprop delta?"
- Hint 3: "For Part C, don't re-derive from scratch — take your `dv1/dτ` expression from Part B and just evaluate it again at the new `v1`, then step again."
- Never: never supply the numeric values of `h`, `o`, `δ_h`, `∂L/∂w1`, or the Part C divergence percentage. Never do the Euler step arithmetic for the learner. Never confirm "yes that's right" before the learner has shown the arithmetic step that produces the number.

## Piece 2 — Simulation: Backprop-vs-PC Update Race

**Type:** simulation · **Time:** 90 min

**Learning goals**
- Implement both learning rules on the same network and confirm the fixed-prediction correspondence empirically, not just symbolically.
- Deliberately break the correspondence via two independent mechanisms (extra settling, added nonlinearity) and measure the break quantitatively.

**Task**

Write a self-contained PyTorch or numpy script, under ~100 lines, with no external data:

1. Build a small MLP (e.g. 2 → 4 → 1, `tanh`) and a handful of synthetic input/target pairs (random, fixed seed).
2. Implement standard backprop: forward pass, `loss.backward()` (or manual gradients), record `∂L/∂w` for every weight.
3. Implement a PC version of the *same* weights: value nodes initialized to the feedforward pass, output clamped to target, inference as gradient descent on the sum-of-squared-errors energy (Euler updates on the hidden value nodes only — weights fixed during inference). Record the weight update `ε_l · f'(pred_l) · v_{l-1}` after `T` inference steps, for `T ∈ {1, 2, 5, 10}`.
4. For each `T`, compute cosine similarity (and/or relative L2 error) between the flattened backprop gradient vector and the flattened PC update vector, across all weights.
5. Break the equivalence a second way: repeat with `T=1` fixed but swap `tanh` for `ReLU` (non-smooth, so the fixed-prediction linearization is a worse approximation even at one step) or with a large inference step size (`τ`-step > 1). Report the same similarity metric.
6. Plot or tabulate similarity vs. `T` (and the nonlinearity/step-size variant), plus 3–5 sentences interpreting the trend in terms of the fixed-prediction assumption from Piece 1.

**Deliverable**

The script (`.py`, runnable, <100 lines excluding comments/imports) plus a short output (printed table or saved plot) and the 3–5 sentence interpretation, submitted together.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | Script runs end-to-end; similarity is high (>0.9 cosine, or comparably small relative error) at `T=1` with small step size and degrades monotonically (or near-monotonically) as `T` increases; the ReLU/large-step variant shows *additional* degradation even at `T=1`; interpretation correctly ties the degradation to "value nodes moving away from the feedforward point" (Piece 1's mechanism), not a vague "PC becomes less accurate over time." |
| Adequate | Script runs and produces a believable trend (general decline in similarity with `T`) but the ReLU/step-size variant is missing, weak, or not clearly separated from the settling-steps effect; interpretation is present but generic. |
| Not yet | Script does not run, hardcodes/fakes the similarity numbers instead of computing them from an actual PC inference loop, or the PC "inference" loop does not actually let error propagate (e.g. never updates hidden value nodes, so it isn't really predictive coding). |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only)

There is no single canonical numeric answer here — a correct implementation's exact similarity numbers depend on network initialization, seed, and step size — but the teacher AI should sanity-check the *shape* of the learner's results against this reference pattern from a correct implementation family:

- At `T=1` inference step with a small step size (`τ ≲ 0.1`) and `tanh`, cosine similarity across all weights should be **≥0.95**, often ≥0.99 for the output-layer weights specifically (which need zero settling to be exact, per Piece 1) and slightly lower for earlier layers.
- As `T` increases past ~5–10 steps (or as step size grows toward `τ≈1`), similarity should visibly decline — expect it to fall into the 0.5–0.9 range by `T=10` for a small MLP, not stay pinned near 1.0. A learner whose similarity stays >0.98 for every `T` up to 10 almost certainly has a bug (e.g. hidden value nodes not actually updating, or weights being updated using the *converged* PC state which happens to still resemble backprop for this particular toy problem — ask them to inspect whether `v1` is actually changing across inference steps).
- The ReLU/large-step variant should show a *visible drop relative to the `tanh`, `T=1`, small-step baseline* — if it doesn't, the learner has likely not actually changed anything that stresses the fixed-prediction linearization (e.g. still using a small step size, or ReLU's kink didn't get exercised because inputs are all positive).
- If a learner reports the trend reversing (similarity going *up* with more settling), that's a red flag worth a hint, not an automatic fail — ask them to check the sign convention on their PC weight-update rule (`ε · f' · presynaptic`, not its negative) before concluding anything about the theory.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: "In your PC inference loop, which variable should change value across the `T` steps? Print it — if it's constant, your loop isn't doing inference."
- Hint 2: "Compare similarity at `T=1` for the output-layer weight specifically versus a hidden-layer weight. Piece 1 predicts these behave differently — does your code show that?"
- Hint 3: "For the ReLU variant, check whether your synthetic inputs ever drive a unit's pre-activation negative. If not, ReLU is behaving like a linear function and nothing will change."
- Never: never provide working PC-inference code or a corrected script. Never state what the "right" similarity number is before the learner has run their own code and reported a number. Point at the mechanism (what should vary, what to print) rather than the fix.

## Piece 3 — Guided Reading: Whittington & Bogacz (2017) + Song et al. (2020)

**Type:** guided reading · **Time:** 75 min

**Learning goals**
- Extract the fixed-prediction assumption's precise statement from W&B's prose, tolerating mangled equations in the source text.
- Extract Z-IL's timing and initialization conditions from Song et al., and what enforcing them costs.
- Distinguish "approximate correspondence" (W&B) from "exact correspondence under conditions X" (Song et al.) as two different claims, not two versions of the same claim.

**Task**

Read (or re-read) `papers/C6_whittington_bogacz_2017_backprop_approximation.txt` and `papers/C6_song_2020_exact_backprop_in_pc.pdf`. Answer the following in your own words, citing the section/paragraph you found each answer in (not an equation number, since the `.txt` file's equations may be garbled from the tag-stripping — locate by surrounding prose instead).

**On Whittington & Bogacz:**
1. The paper names an assumption (their "fixed prediction assumption" or equivalent phrasing) that lets a purely local update rule converge toward the backprop gradient. In your own words: which quantity is held fixed during the inference/relaxation phase, and relative to what point in time (start of inference? every step? the converged fixed point?).
2. Does the paper claim this correspondence holds exactly, or only under a limiting condition (e.g. small inference step, small number of iterations, small learning rate)? Quote or closely paraphrase the sentence that tells you which.
3. Find the paragraph describing the role of "value nodes" and "error nodes." What sign convention does the paper use for the error (prediction minus actual, or actual minus prediction)? Does this match the convention you used in Piece 1?
4. Find where the paper compares its backward-going connections to backprop's backward pass. What specific thing does it say backprop's backward pass requires that PC's backward connections do not?

**On Song et al. (Z-IL):**
5. What does "Z-IL" stand for, and in plain language, what does the "Z" refer to?
6. State the timing condition: at which inference timestep(s) must a given layer's weights be updated for the correspondence to be exact? (Answer in terms of the layer's position/depth, not a specific number unless the paper gives one for a specific example network.)
7. What initialization condition on the value nodes does the paper require for the Z-IL guarantee to hold?
8. The paper's claim is described as "exact." Exact in what sense — read carefully and state precisely what is being claimed to match exactly (a single weight's update? every weight simultaneously? in the limit of infinite network width? for one training example?).
9. What does enforcing the Z-IL timing condition cost or require in practice (e.g., something like a scheduling/clock mechanism per layer, exact knowledge of network depth, a departure from a fully asynchronous or purely local update)? Find the paper's own discussion of this and paraphrase it.

**Perishability check:** this is an actively moving literature (the exact-equivalence result is only a few years old as of this curriculum's writing). Spend 5 minutes checking whether either paper's claim has been superseded, extended, or disputed since 2020–2022 (e.g. via the review paper `C2_millidge_2021_predictive_coding_review.pdf`, which situates both). Note anything relevant in one or two sentences — this is a check, not a literature review.

**Deliverable**

Answers to all 9 questions (2–4 sentences each, with a section/paragraph pointer), plus the perishability check note.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | All 9 answers correct and specific (not generic paraphrase of the abstract); Q2 and Q8 correctly distinguish "approximate, in a limit" (W&B) from "exact, under stated timing/init conditions" (Song); perishability check shows the paper was actually consulted, not just assumed unchanged. |
| Adequate | Most answers correct; Q2/Q8 show awareness that one paper is approximate and one is exact but the stated conditions are vague or slightly wrong; perishability check is present but perfunctory. |
| Not yet | Answers read as generic "PC approximates backprop" restatements without locating the specific held-fixed quantity or the specific timing condition; Q2 and Q8 conflated (treats both papers as making the same strength of claim); perishability check missing. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only)

1. The prediction that a node's own update would otherwise require differentiating through (the top-down error term's dependence on the layer below) is held fixed at its start-of-inference (feedforward-pass) value rather than being recomputed as the value nodes relax — this is what decouples the update into something each unit can compute from its immediate error and pre/post-synaptic activity alone. Matches Piece 1 Part B exactly: `ε1(0)=0` because `v1` starts at the feedforward value.
2. Approximate / limiting condition — the paper's own framing is that the correspondence holds in the regime of a small number of inference iterations (effectively a small-step / early-inference limit), not as an unconditional identity at the converged fixed point. A learner who says "the paper proves PC always equals backprop" has misread it.
3. Error = actual value minus prediction (`ε = v - pred`), same convention used in Piece 1 and consistent with a Hebbian-style update `Δw ∝ ε · presynaptic activity` increasing weight when the postsynaptic value exceeds what was predicted.
4. Backprop's backward pass carries the *derivative/Jacobian* of the loss with respect to activations, computed via the chain rule through the exact forward computational graph (requiring, in the standard formulation, symmetric or transposed weight access — the "weight transport" requirement); PC's backward connections instead carry a locally-computed prediction error, not a transported derivative — no explicit transpose-weight signal needs to travel backward through the whole graph.
5. Z-IL = "Zero-divergence Inference Learning" (some sources render it "Zero-shot"/"exact" inference learning; the key content the learner should state is that "zero" refers to zero *divergence from the backprop gradient*, i.e., the scheme is constructed so the divergence is exactly zero, not merely small). Accept any answer that correctly conveys "zero divergence from backprop," reject "zero" interpreted as "no inference/no iterations" (wrong — Z-IL still requires inference, just with controlled timing).
6. A layer's weights must be updated using the error signal present at the inference timestep that corresponds to that layer's depth/distance from the output (i.e., different layers are updated at different, staggered timesteps during the same inference process, not all at the converged fixed point and not all at `t=0`). The precise indexing (counted from input or from output) is a detail; the essential point the learner must state is "the correct update time is layer-dependent, not a single global time."
7. Value nodes must be initialized to the exact feedforward-pass values (the same condition used in Piece 1 Part B) before inference begins — Z-IL only guarantees exactness relative to that specific initialization, not from an arbitrary starting point.
8. Exact in the sense of: for a given training example, each individual weight's PC update, taken at its layer-specific correct timestep, equals the corresponding backprop gradient exactly (not merely correlated with it, not merely equal in expectation) — this is a per-weight, per-example, per-layer-timestep claim, not a claim about convergence in the infinite-width or infinite-data limit.
9. Enforcing the timing condition requires something that tracks each layer's depth and applies its weight update at the matching, layer-specific inference timestep — effectively an external scheduling/clocking mechanism synchronized to network depth. This is in tension with a maximally local, asynchronous, "just look at your own pre/post/error signal whenever" learning rule: a real synapse would need to "know" which timestep of a global settling process it is currently in, which is extra machinery beyond locality in space (this project's constraint) — it adds a locality-in-time requirement that the project's stated hard constraint does not currently address.

Perishability: acceptable answers note that this remains an active research area (extensions to Z-IL, alternative exact/approximate correspondence results, and critiques of how the correspondence interacts with more complex architectures have continued to appear); the specific new citations found are less important than the learner demonstrating they checked rather than assumed the 2017/2020 results are the final word.

**Teacher constraints**
- Hint 1: "For Q1, don't quote the equation — the text may be garbled. Find the *sentence in prose* that explains what happens during inference before weights update."
- Hint 2: "For Q6, re-read Piece 1 Part B/C. What would it mean, in that toy example, for `w1`'s update to happen 'at the right time' rather than at `τ=0` or at full convergence?"
- Hint 3: "For Q9, the cost isn't computational expense — it's a locality cost. What does a synapse need to 'know' that it wouldn't otherwise need to know?"
- Never: never summarize either paper's abstract as a substitute for the learner reading the relevant section. Never supply the section/paragraph location before the learner has made an attempt. Never resolve the "approximate vs exact" distinction for them directly if their Q2/Q8 answer conflates the two — point them back to re-read both papers' framing sentences instead.

## Piece 4 — Written Defence: Does the Project's Premise Survive PC ≈ Backprop?

**Type:** written defence · **Time:** 75 min

**Learning goals**
- Concede precisely what the equivalence results show before arguing past them.
- Name the specific project features that fall outside every version of the proof (W&B's approximate result and Song et al.'s exact one).
- Give at least one motivation for local PC-style learning that does not reduce to "it computes the same gradient as backprop."
- Correctly and explicitly reject the claim that the equivalence establishes cortical plausibility.

**Task**

Write 500–600 words defending (or honestly reappraising) this project's premise, framed as an argument you would need to make in an MSc research proposal: *why build a local-learning, spiking, graph-topology PC network at all, given that layered PC has been shown to approximately (Whittington & Bogacz) and, under specific conditions, exactly (Song et al.) recover the backprop gradient?*

Your answer must, in some order:
1. State plainly what the equivalence results actually show (concede this before arguing past it — don't bury or soften it).
2. Name one specific step in the W&B derivation and one specific feature of this project's target architecture (Steps 3–5: arbitrary graphs, spiking nonlinearities, stress-triggered structural change, online updates without settling to convergence) that breaks that step. Be specific — "graphs are different from layers" is not specific; naming *which* part of the derivation assumes a feedforward layered order, and why a cyclic graph or a structurally-changing network doesn't have that order, is.
3. Give at least one argument for using PC/local learning that survives even if you accept the equivalence result at face value (e.g. locality itself as the goal — no backward pass to route, no weight-transport requirement, native fit to memristive/neuromorphic hardware, native support for arbitrary and dynamically changing topology, ability to run without ever reaching global convergence).
4. Answer the true/false question directly: does the equivalence prove PC is a plausible model of cortical learning? Justify.

**Deliverable**

500–600 words of prose (word count stated at the end). No bullet-point dodge — acceptance criteria 1–4 above must be visible as argued prose, not a checklist copy-paste.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | Concedes the equivalence accurately and specifically (correctly distinguishes W&B's approximate result from Song et al.'s exact-under-conditions result, per Piece 3); names a real derivation step (e.g. the fixed-prediction assumption's reliance on a well-defined feedforward order / a single settling phase per example) and a real project feature that breaks it (e.g. stress-triggered pruning changes the graph mid-training, so there is no fixed layer depth for Z-IL's timing condition to index into); gives an independent motivation for PC that is not gradient-matching in disguise; correctly answers False on cortical plausibility with a substantive reason (e.g. weight-transport / symmetric-weight assumptions, absence of evidence for the required timing machinery, the proof concerning a feedforward layered graph rather than cortical circuitry). Word count in range. |
| Adequate | Concedes the equivalence but somewhat vaguely (doesn't clearly separate approximate from exact); names a project feature that breaks the equivalence but doesn't tie it to a specific derivation step; independent motivation given but leans partly on restated gradient-matching; correctly answers False but justification is thin ("it's just math, not biology" without saying why that distinction matters here). Word count close to range (450–650). |
| Not yet | Either doesn't concede the equivalence at all (treats it as irrelevant or false), or concedes it and then fails to argue past it (concludes the project is unjustified without engaging acceptance criterion 3), or answers True/unclear on the cortical-plausibility question, or is well outside the word count, or is largely a restatement of Piece 3's reading-question answers without original argument. |

**Grader key** (teacher AI only)

Required content checklist (a strong answer touches all of these; the teacher AI should map the learner's prose onto this list rather than expect a fixed wording):

- **Concession**: W&B — approximate correspondence, holds in a limiting regime (small/early inference relaxation from a feedforward-initialized state); Song et al. — exact correspondence, but only under a specific per-layer timing condition (weight updates staggered by depth) and exact feedforward initialization of value nodes. Neither result says "PC always equals backprop" unconditionally.
- **Specific break**: acceptable examples include — (a) the fixed-prediction assumption and Z-IL's timing condition both presuppose a well-defined, static notion of "layer depth" / feedforward order to index update timing or to define what "the feedforward pass" even is; arbitrary graphs with cycles (Salvatori et al.'s setting, and this project's Steps 3-5) have no such single well-ordered depth, so there is no canonical timestep to assign a given weight's "correct" update time. (b) Z-IL and W&B's derivations assume the network reaches (or is analyzed relative to) a specific point in a single continuous settling process per training example; stress-triggered structural change and online updates without settling to convergence (this project's targets) mean weights update on architectures that are still changing shape mid-inference, or before any settling analogous to "start of inference" is even well-defined. (c) Spiking nonlinearities are non-differentiable / event-based, breaking the smooth-derivative (`f'`) terms both derivations rely on at every step. Any one of these, argued specifically (not just asserted), suffices.
- **Independent motivation** (needs at least one, argued, not just named): locality itself removes the weight-transport problem and the need to route a distinct backward signal through the whole network, which matters independently of what gradient the forward-only local rule happens to compute; native support for topology that changes at runtime (growth/pruning), which a fixed-architecture backprop graph cannot express without re-deriving a new backward graph each time; direct mapping onto memristive/neuromorphic hardware where a physically local update rule is the whole point, independent of whether it exactly matches some other algorithm's gradient; ability to produce a usable weight update from a network that has not settled or converged, relevant for online/continual settings.
- **True/False**: False. Justification must include that the equivalence is a mathematical existence/derivation result about a specific formalism (a feedforward-layered PC graph, with defined value/error nodes, under stated timing/initialization conditions) — it demonstrates that a local rule *can* numerically reproduce a backprop gradient under those conditions, not that biological synapses satisfy those conditions (no evidence offered for the required weight-transport-free symmetric connectivity holding as assumed, no evidence for the Z-IL timing/clocking machinery existing in real synapses, and the base architecture analyzed is not itself a model of any specific cortical circuit). A "False" answer that just says "it's math not biology" without engaging why that distinction bites here is Adequate at best, not Strong.
- Word count: flag if outside ~450–650; a hard fail only below ~350 or above ~750 (task discipline, not content).

**Teacher constraints**
