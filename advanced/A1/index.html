    A1 · Synaptic delays and temporal structure¶

    At a glance

    Length — one to two sittings · about ~3.5 hours across one to two sittings (Piece 1: 60 min, Piece 2: 50 min, Piece 3: 50 min, Piece 4: 45 min) Before this — the Bridge checkpoint; F1 (spike trains), F6 (integrate-and-fire), F9 (STDP) Reading — Thin in D&A — ch. 5 Synaptic Conductances and ch. 6 The Cable Equation give transmission and propagation, but not delay as a computational resource. Primary: Izhikevich 2006, Polychronization: Computation with Spikes. Supporting: Gerstner et al., Neuronal Dynamics (free online) on transmission delays.

    Lesson spec¶

    Goal: Replace the instantaneous weight-matrix picture — spike in, weighted sum out, same tick — with one where when a spike arrives matters as much as how strong it is. Topology plus delays is a computation over time, not just space. Walks away able to distinguish delay-based mechanisms (coincidence detection, delay lines) from weight-based ones.

    Scope — what the lesson covers
    • Real figures: axonal conduction delay (~0.5–10 ms depending on myelination and distance) vs synaptic transmission delay (~0.3–3 ms), and where these numbers come from physically
    • How delay converts a static weight matrix into a temporal computation: coincidence detection and delay lines
    • Delay as a third learnable or evolvable parameter alongside weight and connectivity — what it means for a virtual-memristor synapse to store a delay as well as a weight
    • Polychronous groups (Izhikevich 2006): memory that lives in the delay structure, requiring no weight change
    • Not covered: network-wide rhythms and synchrony (A3), attractor dynamics (A2)

    You should be able to answer: 1. Give typical ms ranges for axonal conduction delay vs synaptic transmission delay, and name one anatomical factor that shortens each. 2. Describe a concrete 2-input circuit where a delay difference — not a weight difference — is what makes a downstream neuron fire. 3. If two synapses feeding the same neuron have delays of 3 ms and 7 ms, under a ±20 ms STDP window, does that 4 ms gap meaningfully change which side of the STDP curve a near-simultaneous pre/post pairing lands on? 4. What does a polychronous group store — weights, delays, or both — and why can one fixed network support many more such groups than it has neurons? 5. Name one way delay could be treated as a plastic parameter like a weight, and one way it is more naturally treated as fixed structural wiring.

    Project tie-in: Feeds Step 3 directly — whether the virtual-memristor synapse carries a delay field alongside its weight, and whether the prediction→error neuron link inside a neuron pair should account for transmission lag.

    Done when: You can sketch a coincidence-detection circuit with explicit delay values and state why polychronous-group memory needs no weight update.

    Coursework¶

    Consume, then apply, then refine:

    audio overview → guided reading (Piece 3) → derivation (Piece 1) → simulation (Piece 2) → written defence (Piece 4)

    Pieces are numbered by type, not by sequence. The reading front-loads the concepts, the derivation makes them exact, the simulation is where they stick and get refined, and the defence is where you find out whether they did.

    Type: derivation · Time: 60 min

    Learning goals - Compute EPSP arrival times from source spike time + transmission delay, and show algebraically that a downstream neuron's firing outcome can flip when only a delay changes, weights held fixed. - Place a delay difference on the STDP timing curve and judge whether it changes which side of the curve (potentiation vs. depression) a pairing lands on, versus merely changing magnitude.

    Task

    Part A — delay flips the outcome, weight does not. Two presynaptic neurons, A and B, both synapse onto neuron C with identical weight w = 0.6 (dimensionless, same units as threshold) and identical EPSP shape: an alpha function EPSP(s) = w · (s/τ) · exp(1 − s/τ) for s ≥ 0, and 0 for s < 0, where s is time since the EPSP's arrival at C and τ = 2 ms. (This alpha function peaks at value w when s = τ — check that you get EPSP(τ) = w before going further.) C fires if the summed potential V(t) = EPSP_A(t − t_arr,A) + EPSP_B(t − t_arr,B) reaches threshold θ = 1.0 at any t. Ignore membrane leak and reset for this derivation — you are only checking whether the sum of two EPSP curves clears the bar.

    A fires at t = 0 ms; total transmission delay (axon + synapse) A→C is 5 ms, so its EPSP arrives at t = 5 ms. B fires at t = 3 ms. Consider two scenarios for B's transmission delay only — A's delay and both weights are unchanged throughout: - Scenario 1: delay B→C = 2 ms → EPSP arrives at t = 5 ms (coincident with A's). - Scenario 2: delay B→C = 6 ms → EPSP arrives at t = 9 ms (4 ms after A's).

    For each scenario, write V(t) as the explicit sum of the two shifted alpha functions, find its maximum value analytically or by evaluating at ≥ 6 well-chosen t values spanning the region where both EPSPs have non-negligible amplitude, and state whether C fires.

    Part B — does a 4 ms delay gap matter to STDP? Now treat A→C (only) as a plastic synapse under a standard exponential STDP rule with a ±20 ms window: Δw = A₊ · exp(−Δt/τ₊) if Δt > 0 (pre-before-post, potentiation), Δw = −A₋ · exp(Δt/τ₋) if Δt < 0 (post-before-pre, depression), with τ₊ = τ₋ = 20 ms and A₊ = A₋ = 1 (normalized units), Δt = t_post − t_pre,arrival.

    A single presynaptic spike at t = 0 reaches C via two candidate synapses with transmission delays 3 ms and 7 ms respectively (so arrivals at t = 3 ms and t = 7 ms). C fires once, at t_post = 10 ms. Compute Δt and Δw for each of the two arrival times. State in one sentence: does the 4 ms delay gap change which side of the STDP curve (potentiation vs. depression) the pairing lands on, or only its magnitude — and by roughly what percentage?

    Deliverable Handwritten or typed: (A) V(t) expressions and evaluated maxima for both scenarios with a fire/no-fire verdict for each; (B) Δt, Δw for both delays and the one-sentence verdict with the percentage.

    Rubric — how this is marked
    Band Criteria
    Strong Part A: correct V(t) construction, correctly locates near-peak by evaluation or calculus, gets both fire/no-fire verdicts right, explicitly states weight was held fixed. Part B: correct Δt and Δw for both delays, correctly identifies both land on the potentiation side, gives a percentage difference within ~5 points of the correct ~22%.
    Adequate Gets both fire/no-fire verdicts right in Part A by evaluation even if the peak-finding is approximate; Part B gets Δt right and correctly says "same side, different magnitude" without an accurate percentage.
    Not yet Flips a fire/no-fire verdict in Part A, or claims in Part B that the 4 ms gap flips potentiation to depression (or vice versa), or conflates the two parts' delay values.

    Type: simulation · Time: 50 min

    Learning goals - Build a minimal leaky-integrate-and-fire (LIF) neuron receiving two synaptic inputs and confirm computationally that varying delay alone — weights and everything else fixed — flips the firing outcome. - Connect the discrete-time simulation result back to the closed-form alpha-function argument in Piece 1 Part A, and be able to say why the two models don't have to agree numerically to agree qualitatively.

    Task Write a self-contained Python script (numpy or PyTorch, your choice; under 100 lines) implementing: - Postsynaptic neuron C, membrane equation dV/dt = −V/τ_m + I(t), τ_m = 10 ms, V(0) = 0, Euler integration with dt = 0.1 ms over T = 20 ms. - Two synaptic input currents, each an exponential PSC I_syn(t) = w · exp(−(t − t_arr)/τ_syn) for t ≥ t_arr (0 otherwise), τ_syn = 1 ms, w = 0.65 for both synapses, held fixed across every run. - Synapse A: arrival always at t_arr,A = 5 ms. - Synapse B: run the simulation twice, changing only the arrival time: (i) t_arr,B = 5 ms ("coincident" — matches the total delay used in Piece 1 Scenario 1), (ii) t_arr,B = 9 ms ("offset" — matches Piece 1 Scenario 2). - Firing threshold V_th = 1.0 (no reset needed — just detect whether V(t) ever reaches V_th and report the peak value and, if fired, the first-crossing time).

    Print, for both runs: peak V, the time of the peak, whether the neuron fired, and (if fired) the crossing time. Your script must produce one run that fires and one that does not, using only the two arrival times above and no other parameter changes between runs.

    Deliverable The script, plus a 3–5 line written note: (a) the two printed results, (b) whether the outcome matches the fire/no-fire pattern from Piece 1 Part A, (c) one sentence on why the numeric peak values differ between the alpha-function derivation and this LIF simulation even though the qualitative pattern matches.

    Rubric — how this is marked
    Band Criteria
    Strong Script runs, uses exactly the specified parameters, correctly reproduces fires=True for the coincident case and fires=False for the offset case, note correctly explains the qualitative match and correctly attributes the numeric mismatch to the different PSP/EPSP model (exponential PSC + leaky integration vs. alpha function), not to a bug.
    Adequate Script runs and gets the right fire/no-fire pattern but the note is thin (just restates the numbers without explaining the model-mismatch point), or minor parameter drift (e.g. dt=1ms) that doesn't change the qualitative result.
    Not yet Script does not reproduce a clean fire/no-fire split (e.g. both fire, or neither fires) without you noticing/investigating, or weights differ between the two runs, or delay values used don't match the two scenarios specified.

    Type: guided reading · Time: 50 min

    Source: Izhikevich, E.M. (2006), Polychronization: Computation with Spikes, papers/A1_izhikevich_2006_polychronization.pdf.

    Learning goals - Extract the paper's own numeric range and construction method for conduction delays, and identify one anatomical/positional factor the paper ties to delay magnitude. - State precisely, in the paper's own terms, what a polychronous group is defined by (weights, delays, or both) and why a fixed network supports far more such groups than it has neurons. - Determine whether the paper treats delays as plastic (co-evolving with STDP) or fixed for the simulation's duration.

    Task — answer these five questions, citing section/figure/table where you found each answer: 1. What integer-millisecond range does the paper assign to synaptic conduction delays in its simulated network, and how is each synapse's specific delay value chosen (fixed at construction? drawn randomly? structured by some spatial/anatomical rule)? 2. In the paper's own words or your close paraphrase, what does it mean for a group of neurons to fire polychronously, as distinct from firing synchronously? Give the distinguishing feature. 3. According to the paper, what is fixed to define a given polychronous group — the set of synaptic weights, the set of delays, or both? Quote or closely paraphrase the specific passage that answers this. 4. The paper argues a fixed network of N neurons can support far more than N coexisting polychronous groups. What mechanism does it give for this (what combinatorial structure is being exploited), and what approximate number of coexisting groups does it report for its example network — and how does that compare to the neuron count used? 5. Does the paper hold conduction delays fixed for the duration of a simulation, or does it let delays change (e.g. via a delay-analogue of STDP) alongside weight changes from STDP? Quote the passage that settles this.

    Deliverable — five short answers (2–5 sentences each), each with a section/figure/table citation from the paper.

    Rubric — how this is marked
    Band Criteria
    Strong All five answers correct with a specific citation each; Q3 and Q4 correctly identify delays (not weights) as the defining/fixed structure of a PNG and correctly state the combinatorial argument (many distinct delay-compatible subsets exist within one fixed network) rather than just asserting "there are more groups than neurons" without the mechanism.
    Adequate Four of five correct with citations; Q4's reported group-count is in the right order of magnitude even if not exact; Q3 correctly says "delays" without fully articulating why weights alone aren't sufficient to define a group.
    Not yet Q3 or Q5 answered backwards (e.g. claims delays are learned via STDP, or that weights alone define a PNG), or no citations given, or Q4 answer just repeats "many more groups than neurons" with no mechanism.

    Type: written defence · Time: 45 min

    Learning goals - Synthesize criteria 3, 4, and 5 into a single argued design decision applicable to Step 3 of the project. - Take an explicit, falsifiable position and defend it against the strongest counterargument, rather than listing pros and cons neutrally.

    Task Write a 300–600 word position paper answering: Should the prediction→error neuron link within a neuron pair (Step 3 of this project) carry a learnable delay parameter, in the same way it carries a learnable weight?

    Take an explicit position — learnable delay, fixed delay set at construction, or some third option (e.g., delay fixed but weight-dependent at construction time, never updated afterward) — and defend it using all three of the following, each addressed explicitly: (a) Where the intra-pair link sits on the axonal (~0.5–10 ms) vs. synaptic (~0.3–3 ms) delay spectrum, and what that implies about the physical/computational role this delay would play if the architecture is meant to be neuromorphic-hardware-compatible. (b) The polychronous-group argument (Piece 3, Izhikevich 2006) for why fixed delays are computationally valuable on their own, without any weight plasticity — and whether that argument transfers to a two-neuron pair, or specifically needs the combinatorics of a larger network to do any work. (c) Whether a local STDP-style rule operating within a ±20 ms window (Piece 1 Part B) is even fine-grained enough to distinguish and act on delay differences of the size relevant to a neuron pair (a few ms) — i.e., could a purely local plasticity rule meaningfully learn a delay at all, given what Piece 1 found about a 4 ms gap only producing a ~22% magnitude change, not a sign flip.

    You must address the strongest counterargument to your own position, not just state your view.

    Deliverable — 300–600 words, plain prose (no bullet-point dumping of (a)/(b)/(c) as a checklist — they should be woven into a single argument).

    Rubric — how this is marked
    Band Criteria
    Strong Takes an explicit, specific position; correctly uses the fact that Izhikevich's PNG argument depends on network-scale combinatorics of many delay-compatible subsets, and explicitly reasons about whether/how that scales down to (or fails to apply to) a single neuron pair; correctly uses Piece 1B's finding (4ms gap → sign-preserving, ~22% magnitude change) to argue about the feasibility of local delay-learning at pair-relevant delay scales; engages a real counterargument and rebuts or concedes it explicitly; stays in the 300–600 word band.
    Adequate Takes a position and uses at least two of (a)/(b)/(c) correctly and specifically (not just gesturing at them); counterargument is present but thin (stated, not really engaged); word count close to the band.
    Not yet No explicit position taken (lists pros/cons and stops), or misuses the PNG argument (e.g. claims it directly implies pair-level delays must be fixed, without addressing the scale mismatch), or ignores Piece 1B's quantitative finding entirely, or no counterargument addressed.

    Verified against the source¶

    Checked against papers/A1_izhikevich_2006_polychronization.pdf rather than recalled. The paper's claim is stronger than "more groups than neurons": with five neurons the example network has 14 polychronous groups, and "adding a sixth neuron triples the number of groups so that there are more groups than synapses in the network". It also states the self-organisation into groups "is a robust phenomenon that occurs despite the experimentalist's efforts to prevent it", and that changing model parameters twofold changes how many groups are supported without eliminating them. Grade the synapse comparison as the correct answer; neurons is the weaker version.

    Completion bar¶

    All four pieces submitted. Piece 1 requires both parts (A and B) with correct fire/no-fire and same-side/magnitude verdicts (Adequate or better). Piece 2's script must reproduce a genuine fire/no-fire split under the specified parameters (Adequate or better) — a script that doesn't split cleanly must be debugged and re-run, not submitted as-is. Piece 3 requires citations for all five answers. Piece 4 requires an explicit position and an engaged counterargument, 300–600 words. Lesson is complete when all four pieces are at Adequate or above; Strong is not required to advance to A2, but any Not-yet piece must be revised once using the hint ladder before moving on.
