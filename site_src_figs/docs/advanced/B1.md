**Size:** one to two sittings

**Goal:** Replace "a success/error chemical is misted over the whole network, each synapse scales by how brightly it is glowing" with what the modulators actually do: distinct signals (reward prediction error vs uncertainty vs gain), released with real spatial and temporal structure, read out with a sign that depends on local receptor type.

**Scope:**
- Dopamine as reward-prediction-error: Schultz's recordings at cue, at reward, and at omission
- ACh and noradrenaline per Yu & Dayan: expected vs unexpected uncertainty, plus gain control on cortical responsiveness
- Volume transmission: real diffusion radius (tens to hundreds of microns) and timescale (hundreds of ms to seconds) vs synaptic transmission, and vs the instantaneous global broadcast a three-factor rule assumes
- Where the abstraction holds (slow, spatially broad, gates plasticity) and where it breaks (multiple co-active modulators, receptor-dependent sign such as D1 vs D2, target-specific innervation density)
- Not covered: astrocytic modulation (already written up), specific interneuron types (B2)

**You should be able to answer:**
1. In Schultz's paradigm, what does a dopamine neuron do at cue, at reward, and at omission of an expected reward — and why is the omission response the strongest evidence against "reward = fixed brightness signal"?
2. Give rough numbers: diffusion radius and timescale of volume transmission vs a synaptic release event.
3. A cue's meaning shifts unpredictably. Which modulator dominates, ACh or NE, and why?
4. Why can the same dopamine concentration increase plasticity at a D1-expressing synapse and decrease it at a D2-expressing one — and what does that break in the "one chemical, one direction" model?
5. State one concrete, specific way your "misted chemical" model is factually wrong about real biology.

**Project tie-in:** Any three-factor-style rule in Steps 3–4, and the stress variable itself, currently assumes a single scalar broadcast. This is the check on whether that is a defensible simplification or needs separate reward-like and uncertainty-like channels before it gates growth and pruning.

**Done when:** Question 5 is answered with a specific correct mechanism — not a restatement of the goal — without further prompting.

## Piece 1 — TD prediction error by hand

**Type:** derivation · **Time:** 60 min

**Learning goals**
- Compute TD(0) prediction errors and value updates by hand across trials, not just write the equation
- See the value signal shift backward in time (reward → cue) as learning proceeds
- Derive, from the update rule itself, why omitting an expected reward produces a *negative* error rather than merely "no signal"
- Connect the arithmetic to Schultz's dopamine recordings via D&A ch. 9's account

**Task**

Two states per trial: cue state **C**, then reward state **R**, then trial ends (terminal value 0). TD(0) rule, with $\gamma=1$, $\alpha=0.5$:

$$\delta(C) = 0 + \gamma V(R) - V(C) \qquad \delta(R) = r_R + \gamma \cdot 0 - V(R)$$

$$V(s) \leftarrow V(s) + \alpha\,\delta(s)$$

$V(C) = V(R) = 0$ initially. Every trial, $r_R = 1$ (reward always delivered) — until trial 6, where $r_R = 0$ (reward omitted, but the cue still fires normally).

Compute, trial by trial for trials 1-6, in this order each trial: $\delta(C)$, then $\delta(R)$, then update both $V(C)$ and $V(R)$. Show your working for at least trials 1, 2, and 6 in full; a table is fine for 3-5.

1. What happens to $\delta(C)$ across trials 1-5? What happens to $\delta(R)$? State the pattern in one sentence and connect it to "the dopamine response migrates from reward to the earliest reliable predictor."
2. Compute $\delta(R)$ on trial 6 (omission) using the $V(R)$ value carried in from trial 5. Compare its sign and magnitude to $\delta(R)$ on trial 1.
3. In one or two sentences: why is the omission result harder to explain under "reward = fixed-brightness success chemical" than the cue and reward results are? (A fixed-brightness model can still shrug off "no glow" at omission — what can't it explain?)

**Deliverable**
A table with columns [Trial, V(C) before, V(R) before, δ(C), δ(R), V(C) after, V(R) after] for trials 1-6, plus written answers to sub-questions 1-3 (a paragraph total, not an essay).

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | All 6 rows arithmetically correct; correctly identifies δ(C) rising toward 1 and δ(R) falling toward 0 as the backward-shift signature; correctly computes δ(R) on trial 6 as strongly negative (not just "smaller than trial 1"); answer to Q3 names the specific gap (a magnitude-only signal has no mechanism to go *below* its resting/no-event level, so it cannot represent "worse than expected," only "how much"). |
| Adequate | Arithmetic correct for at least trials 1-3 and 6 (minor slips elsewhere tolerated); gets the backward-shift direction right but describes it vaguely; gets the omission sign right but the Q3 explanation just restates "it's negative" without saying what that breaks in the fixed-brightness model. |
| Not yet | Arithmetic errors in trial 1 or 2 (wrong sign or wrong update target); reports δ(R) on trial 6 as zero, small-positive, or "no response"; Q3 answer only repeats the lesson's framing back without a mechanism. |

## Piece 2 — Dopamine signatures from a TD agent

**Type:** simulation · **Time:** 105 min · **This is the centrepiece of the lesson.**

**Learning goals**
- Implement TD(0) over a within-trial timeline (not just two lumped states) and watch the value function reshape itself with training
- Reproduce, from a rule the learner wrote and trained themselves, the three canonical dopamine signatures: burst at reward (naive), burst at cue / no response at reward (trained), dip at the omission time (trained, reward withheld)
- Build the intuition that "the response migrates backward in time" is not a story bolted onto TD — it falls out of the Bellman recursion for free

**Task**

Represent one trial as a fixed timeline of $T=20$ discrete steps, cue (CS) always at $t=0$, reward (US) always at $t=T_{US}=10$. Keep a tabular value function $V[0..T-1]$ (initialized to zero) plus one fixed, **never-updated** baseline value $V_{\text{ITI}} = 0$ representing the inter-trial interval (it doesn't predict anything, so nothing should ever push it away from 0 — this is what makes the response appear to originate at the cue rather than drift backward forever).

Per trial:
1. Build $r[t]$: zero everywhere except $r[T_{US}] = 1$ (or $0$ on an omission probe).
2. Compute the "response at cue" as the TD error for the *transition into* the trial: $\delta_{\text{cue}} = 0 + \gamma V[0] - V_{\text{ITI}}$ (this does not update $V_{\text{ITI}}$).
3. Step through $t = 0 \ldots T-1$ in order. At each $t$: $\delta_t = r[t] + \gamma V[t+1] - V[t]$ (using $V[T]:=0$), then update $V[t] \leftarrow V[t] + \alpha\,\delta_t$ immediately (online, not batched).
4. Record $\delta_{\text{cue}}$ and $\delta_t$ at $t=T_{US}$ for every trial you care to inspect.

Run $\geq 300$ training trials with $\gamma=0.98$, $\alpha=0.1$. Then run one **omission probe** trial (do not train on it, or train on a separate copy of $V$) with $r[T_{US}]=0$, and record $\delta_{\text{cue}}$ and $\delta_{T_{US}}$ on that probe.

Report, as printed numbers (a plot is a nice-to-have, not required): $(\delta_{\text{cue}}, \delta_{T_{US}})$ at trial 1, at roughly the halfway point, at the final trained trial, and on the omission probe.

**Deliverable**
A runnable script (numpy or plain PyTorch tensors, no autograd needed, under ~100 lines) plus the four $(\delta_{\text{cue}}, \delta_{T_{US}})$ number-pairs, plus 3-4 sentences stating which pair corresponds to which of the three textbook signatures (naive burst-at-reward; trained burst-at-cue/silent-at-reward; trained-but-omitted dip-at-reward-time) and why $V_{\text{ITI}}$ had to be held fixed for the cue response to show up at all.

**Rubric**

| Band | Criteria |
|---|---|
| Strong | Code runs and reproduces all three signatures within reasonable tolerance (trial 1: δ_cue ≈ 0, δ_reward ≈ 1; final trained: δ_cue clearly positive and δ_reward ≈ 0; omission probe: δ_reward clearly negative, δ_cue ≈ unchanged from final trained value); explanation correctly attributes the cue response's *origin point* to the transition from the fixed $V_{\text{ITI}}=0$, not to $V[0]$ updating itself; correctly notes the omission probe's cue response is unaffected because the agent can't know in advance a given trial will omit. |
| Adequate | Signatures qualitatively present (right signs, right rough trend) but code has a minor bug (e.g. $V[T]$ not treated as 0, or $V_{\text{ITI}}$ accidentally updated causing the cue response to fade); explanation gets 2 of 3 signatures right without the $V_{\text{ITI}}$ mechanism. |
| Not yet | Missing signature (e.g. no dip at omission, or cue and reward responses never separate); $V_{\text{ITI}}$ updated like any other state (this silently destroys the cue-response signature — the giveaway is that the "cue response" keeps growing across arbitrarily many trials instead of converging); no working code, or code that doesn't correspond to the description (batched update instead of online, wrong reward timestep, etc). |

## Piece 4 — Written defence: does the stress variable need more than one channel?

**Type:** written defence · **Time:** 60 min · **300-600 words**

**Learning goals**
- Articulate, without a textbook open, the specific ways real neuromodulation breaks the "one chemical, one direction, instant broadcast" model
- Make a scoped, falsifiable design decision for the project rather than a general essay

**Background the learner needs but that isn't in the available sources** (given here directly, not as an assigned reading, because the papers that would normally carry this — Yu & Dayan 2005 on expected/unexpected uncertainty, and standard pharmacology on D1/D2 signalling — aren't in the source list):
- Volume transmission (how dopamine, ACh, and NE actually reach targets outside direct synapses) diffuses over roughly tens to low hundreds of microns and acts over hundreds of milliseconds to seconds — orders of magnitude slower and more spatially constrained than a fast synaptic release event (sub-micron cleft, sub-millisecond), and very far from an instantaneous, uniform, whole-network mist.
- The standard division of labour: noradrenaline (locus coeruleus) tracks *unexpected* uncertainty — a sudden, unsignalled change in the rules (a cue that used to mean X now unpredictably means Y) — and tends to trigger a global interrupt/gain-reset. Acetylcholine (basal forebrain) tracks *expected* uncertainty — known, stable ambiguity or stochasticity within a model you already trust (a cue you know is only 70% reliable) — and tunes how strongly you weight new evidence against your existing belief, without necessarily discarding that belief.
- Dopamine's effect on plasticity is receptor-dependent in sign: neurons/synapses expressing D1-type receptors are typically pushed one way (associated with the direct pathway, generally facilitating potentiation) by rising dopamine, while D2-expressing neurons/synapses are typically pushed the other way — so the same concentration of the same molecule, at the same time, produces opposite plasticity effects depending on what's listening, not what's broadcasting.

**Task**
Write 300-600 words that:
1. Answer AC2-AC4 in your own words using the background above (don't just restate it — explain why each point is a problem for "one chemical, one direction, instant broadcast," specifically).
2. State one concrete, specific way your original "misted chemical" model is factually wrong about real biology (AC5) — a mechanism, not a restatement of the lesson's goal sentence.
3. Make an explicit decision for the project: does the stress variable (a single scalar per neuron tracking persistent unresolved prediction error, gating growth of new neuron pairs) need to become more than one channel before it's used to gate growth/pruning in Steps 3-4? Justify from the biology above, not from software-engineering taste. If you decide one channel is fine for now, say specifically what it would take to prove you wrong later (what a two-channel need would look like once you see it in simulation).
4. Name one concrete thing that breaks in the current single-scalar stress design if the answer to (3) turns out to be "no, one isn't enough" — e.g. a scenario where a neuron with high stress from an *unpredictable* structural change and a neuron with high stress from *known, stable* noisy input get treated identically by the same growth trigger, when biologically they'd be handled by different systems (NE-style global rewiring interrupt vs. ACh-style local reweighting).

**Deliverable**
300-600 word written defence covering all four points, addressed to a reader who knows the project but hasn't just read this lesson.

| Strong | AC2-AC4 explained with the *implication* for the broadcast model stated explicitly, not just the fact recited; AC5 names a specific mechanism (e.g. omission dip requiring signed subtraction, or receptor-dependent sign, or volume-transmission spatial/temporal scale) rather than re-stating the lesson goal; the project decision in (3) is a real yes/no with a stated falsification condition, not "it depends" or "more research is needed"; (4) gives one concrete scenario, not a generic worry. |
