# A3 · Oscillations and synchrony

!!! abstract "At a glance"

## Lesson spec

**Goal:** Understand how an E-I feedback loop plus conduction delays produces a population rhythm, why different frequencies map to different circuit time constants and functions, and that relative phase — not just rate — can carry information and gate which inputs get through. Closes the gap between "oscillations are a side effect of recurrent circuits" and "oscillations are a functional routing mechanism", with both positions statable.

??? note "Scope — what the lesson covers"

    - PING vs ING: how the E→I→E loop and its time constants set cycle period; why inhibition, not excitation, paces the rhythm
    - Bands with real numbers: theta ~4–8 Hz (long loop, navigation and sequence memory) vs gamma ~30–80 Hz (short local loop, feature binding and attention)
    - Communication-through-coherence: spikes arriving inside vs outside a receiving neuron's excitability window
    - The binding problem and phase alignment across assemblies
    - Phase-of-firing as an information channel additional to rate
    - Not covered: replay and sharp-wave ripples (B5), attractor fixed points (A2)

**You should be able to answer:**
1. Given excitatory decay ~5 ms, inhibitory decay ~10 ms and a 2 ms loop delay, estimate whether the rhythm lands in gamma or theta, and why.
2. Why does a short local E-I loop produce gamma while a longer loop produces theta — what specifically about loop delay changes?
3. In a 25 ms gamma cycle with a 5 ms postsynaptic excitability window, work out whether two inputs 12 ms apart in phase both get through, and what determines the answer.
4. Give a concrete example of information carried in phase-of-firing that could not be recovered from rate alone.
5. State the strongest evidence that oscillations are causally functional, and the strongest evidence they are epiphenomenal — one piece each.

**Project tie-in:** Bears on Step 3 — whether coordination across graph nodes needs an explicit phase/synchrony mechanism, or whether the purely-local constraint (no global clock) can achieve equivalent routing without a shared oscillatory reference.

**Done when:** You sketches the PING loop timing diagram unprompted, places theta and gamma with their functions, and gives one concrete piece of evidence for each side of the functional-vs-epiphenomenal debate.

=== "Piece 1 — PING loop timing and the coherence window"

    **Type:** derivation · **Time:** 60–75 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Derive an E-I loop's oscillation period from its inhibitory decay and loop delay, and show excitatory decay does not enter.
    - Explain mechanistically why inhibition, not excitation, paces a PING rhythm.
    - Compute whether two phase-separated inputs both fall inside a receiving neuron's excitability window, and identify what that outcome depends on.

    **Task**

    **Part A — period from decay + delay.**
    Model: an excitatory population E and inhibitory population I in a feedback loop. E fires a volley; after one-way conduction delay `d` it reaches I; I fires and releases inhibition that decays with time constant `tau_I`; once that inhibition has decayed, and after a further one-way delay `d` for the inhibitory signal to reach E, E is free to fire the next volley. Use the approximation

    ```
    T ≈ tau_I + 2d
    f = 1000 / T      (T in ms, f in Hz)
    ```

    (the `2d` is the round-trip conduction delay: once E→I, once I→E; `tau_I` is the time inhibition takes to decay enough for E to escape).

    Given parameters:
    - `tau_E` = 5 ms (excitatory synaptic/membrane decay)
    - `tau_I` = 10 ms (inhibitory decay)
    - `d` = 2 ms (one-way loop delay)

    1. Compute `T` and `f` from the formula. State whether the rhythm lands in theta (~4–8 Hz) or gamma (~30–80 Hz).
    2. `tau_E` = 5 ms was given but does not appear in the formula. Explain in 2–3 sentences, in terms of the PING mechanism, why the excitatory decay constant does not set the inter-volley interval, while the inhibitory decay constant does.
    3. Recompute `T` and `f` for a "long loop": `tau_I` = 100 ms, `d` = 15 ms. State the resulting band.
    4. Sketch (ASCII is fine) a timing diagram of one full cycle of the case in (1): a time axis in ms from 0 to T, with marks for "E fires," "signal reaches I," "I fires / inhibits," "inhibition reaches E and starts decaying," and "E free to fire again." Every mark must have a real ms value from your computation, not a placeholder.

    **Part B — the coherence window.**
    A gamma rhythm has a 25 ms cycle. The receiving neuron has a 5 ms window of postsynaptic excitability once per cycle (the period right after inhibition bottoms out, when the membrane is close enough to threshold for an incoming EPSP to trigger a spike). Two excitatory inputs, A and B, are phase-locked to two different upstream assemblies; B arrives 12 ms later in phase than A on every cycle.

    5. If A arrives inside the excitability window, work out whether B also arrives inside a window (same cycle or a neighbouring one). Show the arithmetic (12 ms vs. window width 5 ms vs. cycle length 25 ms).
    6. State explicitly what determined your answer to (5) — is it the number of spikes, the firing rate of A and B, or something else?

    **Deliverable:** one page (typed or scanned handwriting) with: Part A steps 1–4 (including the diagram), Part B steps 5–6, one line of computed numbers per step — no unexplained final answers.

    ??? info "Rubric — how this is marked"

        | Band | Criteria |
        |---|---|
        | Strong | Both Part A cases correctly classified (gamma, theta) with correct T/f arithmetic; explanation of why `tau_E` drops out is mechanistic (references the loop's "brake" being inhibition, not "E is ready to fire" hand-waving); timing diagram has all four marked events with correct ms values; Part B correctly concludes B is filtered out with reasoning tied to phase modulo cycle length, not rate. |
        | Adequate | Correct band classification in both Part A cases but the "why `tau_E` drops out" explanation is vague or only restates the formula; diagram present but missing one mark or one value; Part B has the right numeric conclusion but the "what determines the answer" line is hand-wavy or conflates phase with rate. |
        | Not yet | Either Part A case misclassified (e.g. calls the 5/10/2 case theta or the 100/15 case gamma); no mechanistic account of why `tau_E` is irrelevant; Part B concludes "both get through" or "neither gets through" without correct 12 vs. 5 vs. 25 arithmetic. |

=== "Piece 2 — E-I rate-model rhythm and its onset"

    **Type:** simulation · **Time:** 75–100 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Build a minimal delayed-feedback E-I rate model and measure its emergent oscillation frequency numerically, without assuming the answer.
    - Empirically confirm the direction of the tau_I → frequency relationship predicted in Piece 1.
    - Observe that oscillation is not guaranteed for every parameter setting — there is an onset/offset boundary (ties to D&A ch.7's treatment of when the E-I system transitions from a stable fixed point to a sustained rhythm).

    **Task**

    Implement, in Python (numpy or PyTorch, no autograd needed), a two-population rate model with an explicit conduction delay, Euler-integrated:

    ```
    dE/dt = ( -E + sigmoid( gain * (w_EE*E   - w_EI*I_delayed + I_ext - theta_E) ) ) / tau_E
    dI/dt = ( -I + sigmoid( gain * (w_IE*E_delayed - w_II*I           - theta_I) ) ) / tau_I
    ```
    where `E_delayed(t) = E(t - d)`, `I_delayed(t) = I(t - d)` (implement the delay with a ring buffer / array indexing, not an approximation), `sigmoid(x) = 1/(1+exp(-x))`.

    **Exact fixed parameters** (do not change these):
    `tau_E = 5 ms`, `d = 2 ms`, `w_EE = 2.0`, `w_EI = 2.0`, `w_IE = 2.0`, `w_II = 0.0`, `I_ext = 1.0`, `theta_E = 1.0`, `theta_I = 1.0`, `gain = 10.0`, `dt = 0.1 ms`, `T_total = 1000 ms`, initial condition `E[0..d] = 0.3`, `I[0..d] = 0.1`.

    **Sweep:** run the model once per value of `tau_I ∈ {5, 10, 20, 30, 50, 65, 70} ms`, all other parameters fixed as above.

    **Measurement:** discard the first 400 ms as transient. On the remaining `E(t)` trace, count rising zero-crossings of `E(t) - mean(E(t))` and compute the mean inter-crossing interval as the period; frequency = 1000/period (Hz). If fewer than 3 crossings are found in the retained window, report "no sustained oscillation" for that `tau_I` rather than a spurious frequency.

    **Deliverable:**
    1. The script (under ~100 lines).
    2. A table or plot of frequency (Hz) vs. `tau_I` (ms) for all 7 sweep values.
    3. 3–5 sentences: (a) does frequency increase or decrease with `tau_I`, matching or contradicting Piece 1's prediction that longer `tau_I` → lower frequency? (b) what happens at the high end of the sweep, and why is that a real result and not a bug? (c) explicitly note that the exact Hz values will NOT match Piece 1's `T ≈ tau_I + 2d` formula number-for-number — explain in one sentence why not (different model: saturating/nonlinear feedback vs. the linear round-trip approximation), while the *direction* of the trend should still agree.

    ??? info "Rubric — how this is marked"

        | Band | Criteria |
        |---|---|
        | Strong | Correct delay-buffer implementation (verified by delay ≠ 0 having an effect); frequency strictly decreases as `tau_I` increases across the range where oscillation exists; correctly reports "no sustained oscillation" at `tau_I = 70`ms (or wherever it empirically stops in their run) rather than reporting a fabricated near-zero frequency; the three explanatory sentences are all present and (b) correctly frames the disappearance as a stability transition (fixed point becomes stable / Hopf-type boundary), not a numerical artifact. |
        | Adequate | Correct qualitative trend and correct script, but the write-up conflates "no oscillation" with "very low frequency," or the explanation in (c) is missing or superficial ("numbers are different because it's a different model" with no reason why). |
        | Not yet | Frequency trend is flat, increasing, or noisy/non-monotonic (implementation bug — likely delay not applied, or measurement window too short); no acknowledgment that oscillation can disappear; script silently reports frequency = 0 or NaN without comment. |

=== "Piece 3 — D&A ch.7 guided reading"

    **Type:** guided reading · **Time:** 45–60 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Ground Piece 1's period formula in D&A's own coupled excitatory-inhibitory rate equations.
    - Locate, in a primary source, the actual condition D&A gives for an E-I network to transition from a stable fixed point to sustained oscillation.
    - Identify explicitly what D&A does and does not cover about phase/timing, so you knows the boundary of the source material before Piece 4.

    **Task**

    Read D&A, Chapter 7, the section(s) on excitatory-inhibitory network dynamics (the part that sets up coupled rate equations for an E and an I population and analyzes their stability). Answer:

    1. Write down D&A's coupled rate equations for the E and I populations (their notation, not Piece 1's). Map each of D&A's coupling terms onto Piece 1's `w_EE`, `w_EI`, `w_IE`, `w_II`.
    2. Find the condition D&A states for the coupled system to move from a stable fixed point to a sustained oscillation. Write it in your own words. Does it talk about eigenvalues, a trace/determinant condition, or something else?
    3. In Piece 1 Part A, `w_II = 0` yet the loop still oscillated because of the E↔I cross-coupling. Does D&A's stability condition depend on self-inhibition (`w_II`), or purely on the cross terms (`w_EI`, `w_IE`) and the time constants? State which, citing the relevant equation/expression.
    4. D&A's basic E-I model has no explicit axonal conduction delay term (unlike Piece 1's `d`). Note one place where D&A comes close to discussing timing/delay between populations, or explicitly note "no such discussion found in this section" if that's the case — do not invent one.
    5. Find one place where D&A connects the E-I network's dynamics to a concrete phenomenon (e.g., response to a step or ramp input, contrast-invariant tuning, or similar). State it in one sentence.

    **Deliverable:** half a page, five numbered short answers, each citing the specific equation number or a clear paraphrase location (e.g. "the paragraph introducing the linearized Jacobian of the coupled rate equations") since page numbers vary by printing.

=== "Piece 4 — Functional or epiphenomenal?"

    **Type:** written defence · **Time:** 45–60 min · **300–600 words**

    **Learning goals**
    - State a concrete example of phase-carried information that rate alone cannot recover.
    - Marshal one piece of evidence each for "oscillations are functionally causal" and "oscillations are an epiphenomenon of recurrent E-I dynamics."
    - Take an explicit, falsifiable position on whether Step 3's purely-local, no-global-clock constraint can substitute for an oscillatory phase reference — and say what evidence would change you's mind.

    **Task**

    Write 300–600 words that do all of the following:

    1. Give one concrete, specific example of information that is carried in the *phase* of firing relative to a population rhythm and that could not be recovered from firing rate alone (rate = spike count in a window, with phase information discarded). Use a real quantity (e.g. a phase value in degrees or ms relative to cycle, mapped to something it encodes) — not just "phase carries information."
    2. State the single strongest piece of evidence you can identify (from D&A ch.7, your Piece 1 derivation, your Piece 2 simulation, or the available papers list — C1–C6, B2, B5) that oscillations are causally functional (the rhythm itself is used downstream, not just a byproduct).
    3. State the single strongest piece of evidence, from the same source pool, that oscillations can be epiphenomenal (a byproduct of E-I feedback dynamics with no computational role attributed to the rhythm itself). Your own Piece 2 simulation — a toy E-I loop that oscillates purely as a consequence of feedback and delay, without any "use" being defined for the rhythm — is a legitimate example here if you argue it correctly.
    4. Take a position on the project tie-in: does Step 3's hard constraint (all learning purely local — pre-synaptic activity, post-synaptic activity, local error signal; no global loss; no global clock) rule out relying on an emergent oscillatory phase reference for coordinating graph nodes, make it necessary, or leave it irrelevant? Argue for one of these three, using your Piece 1/2 results as evidence (e.g., is a shared phase reference something that could emerge locally from many small E-I loops without a global clock, or does synchrony of that kind require exactly the kind of global timing signal the constraint forbids?).
    5. State one specific observation or result that would change your mind on point 4 — not "more evidence" in general, but a stated, checkable thing (e.g. "if two unconnected local E-I loops in a graph topology spontaneously phase-lock without any shared input or connection between them, that would suggest synchrony is achievable without a global clock").

    **Deliverable:** 300–600 words, five clearly labeled parts matching 1–5 above (a numbered list or headers is fine — flowing prose is not required to hit all five points explicitly).

    ??? info "Rubric — how this is marked"

        | Band | Criteria |

        | Adequate | All five points addressed but (2)/(3) are generic ("oscillations seem important" / "the sim just did it automatically") rather than a specific named source or result; (4) states a position but doesn't connect to the derivation/sim; (5) is vague ("if I saw more evidence"). |
        | Not yet | Missing one or more of the five points; (1) restates "phase carries information" without a concrete example; (4) refuses to take a position ("it depends" with no argument); (5) is unfalsifiable or absent. |

## Completion bar

- Sketch a PING loop timing diagram (E fires → delay → I fires → inhibition decays → delay → E fires again) with plausible real ms values, not just labeled arrows.
- Place theta (~4–8 Hz, long loop, navigation/sequence memory) and gamma (~30–80 Hz, short loop, feature binding/attention) correctly, and explain the placement in terms of loop delay and inhibitory decay, not just memorized numbers.
