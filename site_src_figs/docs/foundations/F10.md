## Lesson spec

**Goal:** Establish reinforcement learning as a third paradigm distinct from supervised and unsupervised: learning driven by a scalar evaluative signal rather than a target output or input statistics. Derive Rescorla-Wagner from the phenomena it must explain, extend to TD learning for within-trial timing, then to action selection.

??? note "Scope — what the lesson covers"

    - The behavioural phenomena — acquisition, extinction, blocking, overshadowing, secondary conditioning — and why blocking rules out contiguity as the mechanism
    - The Rescorla-Wagner rule derived, worked trial by trial, and shown to account for blocking, overshadowing and extinction with one equation
    - TD learning: the TD error defined over within-trial time, with its value at cue, at reward and at reward omission derived rather than asserted
    - Static action choice: exploration versus exploitation, and the indirect actor
    - Sequential action choice: the actor-critic architecture, policy evaluation and improvement, and how the critic bootstraps from its own future prediction
    - Not covered: dopamine recordings and where the TD abstraction empirically breaks (B1)

**You should be able to answer:**
1. For a cue-reward pairing with learning rate 0.2, asymptote 1 and initial value 0, fill in a four-trial table of value and prediction error before and after each trial.
2. In a blocking experiment — A trained to reward, then AB trained to reward — what associative strength does Rescorla-Wagner assign to B, and which term in the update forces that result?
3. With a cue at t = 0 and reward at t = 5, at which timesteps is the TD error nonzero before learning converges, and what is it at reward once the value function has converged?
4. In actor-critic, which equation does the critic use to update its value estimate, and what quantity from that update is handed to the actor?
5. How does the indirect actor's action-value update differ mechanically from a direct policy update?

**Feeds:** B1, which takes the TD error defined here as the candidate account of dopamine firing and tests it against recorded data, including where the single-scalar abstraction fails.

**Done when:** You derives Rescorla-Wagner and TD updates from raw trial data by hand, and attributes blocking, overshadowing and actor-critic bootstrapping to specific terms in those equations.

=== "Piece 3 — Guided reading: D&A ch. 9"

    **Type:** guided reading · **Time:** 45-60 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Acquire the vocabulary of classical conditioning phenomena precisely enough to use it in a derivation.
    - Understand why blocking is the phenomenon that forces prediction-based (not contiguity-based) learning.
    - Distinguish the actor's role from the critic's role before meeting their equations.

    **Task**

    Read, in order: D&A ch. 9 *Introduction*, *Classical Conditioning*, *Static Action Choice*, *Sequential Action Choice*. Answer the following, citing the section (not a page number) that supports each answer:

    1. (*Introduction*) What distinguishes reinforcement learning from supervised and unsupervised learning as a third paradigm? What is the scalar signal that drives learning, and what does it lack compared to a supervised target?
    2. (*Classical Conditioning*) Define, in D&A's terms (CS, US): acquisition, extinction, blocking, overshadowing, secondary conditioning. One to two sentences each.
    3. (*Classical Conditioning*) Why does blocking — not acquisition or extinction alone — rule out simple contiguity (mere co-occurrence of CS and US) as the learning mechanism? What must the learning rule be sensitive to instead?
    4. (*Classical Conditioning*) What does D&A mean by "prediction error," and how is the Rescorla-Wagner rule stated in terms of the *total* predicted reward from all stimuli present on a trial (not just one stimulus in isolation)?
    5. (*Static Action Choice*) What is the exploration-exploitation trade-off? How does the softmax (Boltzmann) action-selection rule implement a compromise between the two, and what does its temperature-like parameter control?
    6. (*Static Action Choice*) What does D&A call the "indirect actor," and indirect *with respect to what*?
    7. (*Sequential Action Choice*) In the actor-critic architecture, which component performs policy evaluation and which performs policy improvement?
    8. (*Sequential Action Choice*) What does it mean for the critic's value estimate to "bootstrap" — i.e., to update V(s_t) using V(s_{t+1}) rather than only a final observed reward? Why is this useful before a trial or episode has finished?

    **Deliverable**

    Numbered answers to all eight questions, each naming its section, 2-5 sentences per answer. No equations required here (Piece 1 does the formal work) — plain-language understanding only.

    ??? info "Rubric — how this is marked"

        | Band | Criteria |
        |---|---|
        | Strong | All eight answered correctly and specifically; Q3 and Q4 correctly identify that RW's error term is computed from the *sum* of associative strengths of all present stimuli, not per-stimulus contiguity; Q6-Q8 correctly separate actor from critic and value-learning from action-selection. |
        | Adequate | Six or more correct; minor vagueness in the mechanism of Q3/Q4 (e.g. says "blocking shows contiguity is wrong" without saying what replaces it) or Q7/Q8 (right roles, fuzzy on bootstrapping). |
        | Not yet | Fewer than six correct, or any answer that defines a phenomenon (Q2) backwards (e.g. swaps overshadowing and blocking), or misses that the RW error is a *summed* prediction. |

=== "Piece 1 — Four formal machines, worked by hand"

    **Type:** derivation · **Time:** 90-120 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Derive Rescorla-Wagner trial dynamics from raw numbers, not from the closed-form solution.
    - Show mechanically, with numbers, which term in the update produces blocking.
    - Derive (not assert) which within-trial timesteps carry nonzero TD error, before and after convergence.
    - State the critic and actor update equations and identify exactly what quantity couples them.

    **Task**

    **Part A — Rescorla-Wagner acquisition (AC1).** A single CS is paired with a US of reward magnitude r = 1 every trial. Initial associative strength V₀ = 0, learning rate α = 0.2. The Rescorla-Wagner rule is:
    δₙ = r − Vₙ (prediction error on trial n, computed *before* the update)
    Vₙ₊₁ = Vₙ + α·δₙ
    By hand, fill in V (before), δ, and V (after) for trials 1-4.

    **Part B — Blocking (AC2).** Stimulus A is trained alone for 4 trials under the same rule (α = 0.2, r = 1) — reuse Part A's numbers, so V_A after 4 trials is known. Then compound AB training begins: V_B starts at 0, and on every AB trial the *shared* prediction error is computed from the sum:
    δₙ = r − (V_A,ₙ + V_B,ₙ)
    ΔV_A = ΔV_B = α·δₙ (both present stimuli get the identical increment, since both are present and equally salient)
    By hand, compute V_A, V_B, and δ for AB trials 1-4 (i.e., trials 5-8 overall). Then answer: what stays constant across every AB trial, and why does that quantity determine how much blocking occurs? What would V_A and V_B converge to if A had instead been pretrained for 30 trials before AB began (state the limiting logic; you do not need to hand-compute 30 trials)?

    **Part C — TD error within a trial (AC3).** A trial has six timesteps, t = 0 (cue onset) through t = 5 (reward delivery, r = 1; r = 0 at all other t). Value is represented as one weight per timestep, wₜ = V(t), all initialized to 0. Terminal value V(6) = 0 (end of trial). Learning rate α = 0.3, discount γ = 1. The TD rule, applied at every timestep within every trial:
    δₜ = rₜ + γ·V(t+1) − V(t)
    V(t) ← V(t) + α·δₜ (only the active timestep's weight updates)
    By hand, compute δ₀...δ₅ and the resulting weights after trial 1, then after trial 2. State in words what happens to the *location* of the nonzero error as trials 1, 2, 3, ... proceed, and state what δ at t = 5 equals once V has converged (give the exact converged weights, not just "it's zero because it's zero" — show why).

    **Part D — Actor-critic and the indirect actor (AC4, AC5).**
    (i) Write the critic's update equation for V(sₜ) in an actor-critic architecture, and state which single quantity from that equation is also handed to the actor.
    (ii) Indirect actor: for a two-action choice with action values m₁ = m₂ = 0, action 1 is chosen and reward r = 1 is received. Using update m_a ← m_a + α(r − m_a) with α = 0.3, compute m₁ after the trial. Using softmax P(a) = exp(β·m_a)/Σ_b exp(β·m_b) with β = 2, compute the resulting P(a₁) and P(a₂) (to 4 decimal places).
    (iii) Direct actor: for the same trial, using a direct probability update p_a ← p_a + α·r·(1 − p_a) for the chosen action and p_b ← p_b − α·r·p_b for the other, with initial p₁ = p₂ = 0.5 and α = 0.3, compute p₁ and p₂ after the trial. Confirm p₁ + p₂ = 1 still holds and explain in one sentence why this update needs no separate softmax step while the indirect actor's does.

    **Deliverable**

    A single document (handwritten photo, scan, or typed) with all four parts' tables/computations shown in full — every intermediate number, not just final answers — plus the short written answers requested in B, C, and D(iii).

    ??? info "Rubric — how this is marked"

        | Band | Criteria |
        |---|---|
        | Strong | All four parts arithmetically correct to the values in the grader key (small rounding tolerance ±0.001 acceptable); Part B correctly identifies that V_A − V_B (the gap at the start of AB training) is invariant across AB trials and correctly reasons the full-blocking limit as V_A(pretrain) → 1; Part C correctly states the converged weights are all 1 and shows why δ₅ = 0 there (not just "V is right so error is zero" — ties it to r + γV(6) − V(5) = 1 + 0 − 1); Part D(i) correctly names δₜ as the shared quantity; D(iii) correctly explains the mechanical difference between indirect (value estimate + separate softmax) and direct (probability updated in one step). |
        | Adequate | Parts A and C's core arithmetic correct; Part B's numbers correct but the "why constant" reasoning is hand-wavy; Part D done with correct formulas but an arithmetic slip in the softmax or direct-actor computation. |
        | Not yet | Arithmetic errors compound across trials (e.g. forgot to use the *updated* V for the next trial's δ); Part B treats blocking as "B just doesn't get trained" without identifying the shared-error mechanism; Part D confuses which quantity the critic hands the actor. |

=== "Piece 2 — Simulation: the TD error moves backward, and blocking emerges from one shared term"

    **Type:** simulation · **Time:** 60-90 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Watch, from your own code's output, the TD error's peak shift backward from reward to cue across trials — not read about it.
    - Watch the omission dip appear when an expected reward fails to arrive.
    - Verify blocking numerically: train A alone, then train the AB compound, and confirm B's associative strength stays suppressed relative to an unblocked control.

    **Task**

    Write one self-contained Python file (standard library + numpy + matplotlib only, no downloads), under 100 lines, in two parts.

    **Part A — TD signatures.** Implement the within-trial TD update from Piece 1 Part C exactly: 6 timesteps per trial (t=0 cue, t=5 reward), one weight per timestep (CSC representation), α=0.3, γ=1.0, terminal V=0. Run 200 trials with reward r=1 always delivered at t=5, recording the full δ vector every trial. Then:
    - Plot δₜ (y-axis) vs. t (x-axis) as separate lines/curves for a handful of trials spanning early to late training (e.g. trials 1, 2, 3, 5, 10, 20, 50) on one axes, with a legend — the plot must make the backward shift of the error's peak visually obvious.
    - After the 200 training trials, run one additional trial with the reward *omitted* (r=0 at t=5) using the converged weights, and print the resulting δ vector, highlighting the value at t=5.

    **Part B — Blocking, verified numerically.** Implement Rescorla-Wagner with the shared, summed error exactly as in Piece 1 Part B: two conditions, both α=0.2, r=1.
    - *Blocked condition*: train stimulus A alone for 30 trials, then train compound AB for 20 further trials (V_B starts at 0; every AB trial computes one shared δ = r − (V_A+V_B) and applies it to both).
    - *Control condition*: train compound AB from scratch (both at 0) for 20 trials, no pretraining.
    - Print final V_A and V_B for both conditions.

    **Deliverable**

    The `.py` file, the plot (saved as PNG or shown), and the four printed final values (blocked V_A, blocked V_B, control V_A, control V_B) with a two-sentence caption stating whether the blocked V_B is meaningfully suppressed relative to the control V_B.

    ??? info "Rubric — how this is marked"

        | Band | Criteria |
        |---|---|
        | Strong | Plot clearly shows the δ peak at t=5 for early trials shifting to earlier t across the listed trial snapshots (visually, not just numerically); omission trial correctly prints a negative δ at t=5 with converged weights; blocking numbers land within ~0.01 of the grader key (V_A≈0.9994, V_B≈0.0006 blocked; V_A≈V_B≈0.50 control) and caption correctly states blocking is essentially complete. |
        | Adequate | Numbers close to the grader key but plot is present yet cluttered/mislabeled (backward shift technically visible but not "obvious"); omission dip computed correctly but not clearly printed/highlighted. |
        | Not yet | Weights updated using the *pre-update* V(t+1) inconsistently (order-of-update bug) producing a δ pattern that doesn't shift backward; blocking implemented with independent (non-shared) error terms per stimulus, producing no blocking at all (V_A≈V_B≈1 in the "blocked" condition) — this is the most common conceptual bug and should be flagged specifically. |

=== "Piece 4 — Written explanation"

    **Type:** written explanation · **Time:** 30-45 min

    **Learning goals**
    - Articulate reinforcement learning as a paradigm distinct from supervised and unsupervised learning, in terms of the information the scalar reward signal carries and lacks.
    - Connect the specific mathematical terms derived in Pieces 1-2 (the summed error in RW, the bootstrapped error in TD) to the specific phenomena they explain (blocking, backward-shifting prediction, actor-critic bootstrapping).

    **Task**

    In 300-600 words, explain: (a) why reinforcement learning is a third paradigm alongside supervised and unsupervised learning — what the reward signal gives you and what it withholds; (b) how one equation, the Rescorla-Wagner update with its *summed* prediction error, is enough to produce both ordinary acquisition and blocking, and why the same equation without the sum (independent per-stimulus errors) would fail to produce blocking; (c) how TD extends this to *within-trial* timing by bootstrapping V(t) from V(t+1), and why this bootstrapping is exactly what makes the prediction error's peak shift backward across trials; (d) how the actor-critic architecture reuses the same TD error as the signal for policy improvement, not just value learning.

    **Deliverable**

    300-600 words of prose (no code, equations allowed inline where they clarify a point but the piece should read as explanation, not as a repeat of Piece 1's derivations).
