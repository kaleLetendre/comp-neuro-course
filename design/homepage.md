# Computational Neuroscience

A free, self-paced course in computational neuroscience, built around Dayan & Abbott's *Theoretical Neuroscience* (MIT Press, 2001) and the exercise sets the authors publish alongside it. No enrollment, no instructor. 28 lessons, about 135 hours.

Eleven foundation lessons work through the textbook chapter by chapter: spike trains and firing rates, receptive fields, neural decoding, information theory, membrane biophysics, integrate-and-fire neurons, cable theory, network models, synaptic plasticity, reinforcement learning, and representational learning. A synthesis checkpoint closes the track. Sixteen advanced lessons follow, in three tracks: spiking network dynamics, biology for modellers, and predictive coding.

Each lesson pairs a lecture with a spec of acceptance criteria and four pieces of coursework: guided reading, a pen-and-paper derivation, a simulation under 100 lines, and a written explanation. Where the textbook authors publish their own exercises for a chapter, those take precedence over the course's own. Every simulation in the foundation track was run before its answers were written — the numbers in the lectures are measured, not plausible.

Needs linear algebra, calculus, probability, and the ability to write a simulation. No neuroscience background assumed.
