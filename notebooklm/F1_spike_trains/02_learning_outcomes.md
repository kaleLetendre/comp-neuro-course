# F1 learning outcomes — what the listener should be able to do afterwards

This document tells the generator what the lesson is for. The audio should build toward these capabilities.

By the end the learner should be able to:

1. **Compute a spike-count rate** from a raw spike train, and say what additional data would be needed to estimate a time-dependent rate at a particular instant.
2. **Use the Fano factor as a diagnostic** — know what value a Poisson process gives, and reason about what a departure in either direction implies about the underlying spiking.
3. **Choose a smoothing window and defend it**, articulating what a wide window costs and what a narrow one costs, and why no choice escapes both.
4. **Read a tuning curve**, saying what its peak and its width each mean operationally.
5. **State the rate-versus-timing question as genuinely open**, with one concrete piece of evidence on each side.

## Important constraint for generation

These outcomes are also the lesson's assessment questions. **Do not answer them directly and do not present them as a quiz.** Teach the mechanisms they depend on so a listener who has understood the material can construct the answers. Explaining what the Fano factor measures and why a refractory period imposes regularity is correct; announcing "so the answer is below 1" is not.
